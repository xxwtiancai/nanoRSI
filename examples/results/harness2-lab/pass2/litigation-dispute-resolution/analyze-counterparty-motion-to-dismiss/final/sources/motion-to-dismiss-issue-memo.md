## I. Executive Summary

DataCore's omnibus motion is vulnerable on nearly every ground. The strongest opposition arguments are:

1. **Personal jurisdiction (12(b)(2)):** The Complaint alleges specific jurisdiction based on DataCore's Atlanta regional office (100 Techwood Drive NW), ~14 Georgia employees, and misrepresentations made *from Georgia* by VP Victor Sousa, who also signed the MSA from the Atlanta office. This satisfies Georgia's long-arm statute and due process under *Burger King*. Notably, the Whitford Declaration conspicuously **omits any denial of the Atlanta office or Sousa's Georgia presence** — it describes only Austin operations — which we should exploit.
2. **Forum selection clause (12(b)(3)):** Venue enforcement of a forum-selection clause runs through § 1404(a) transfer (the *Atlantic Marine* framework), not Rule 12(b)(3) dismissal; venue is facially proper under § 1391(b)(2). Even under § 1404, the clause covers only disputes "arising out of or relating to this Agreement," and the fraud-in-the-inducement claims target pre-contract conduct; there is no fraud-unraveling (carve-in) clause.
3. **Contractual limitations period (§ 9.4):** When Pinnacle "knew or should have known" of the breach is a fact question — DataCore's own CTO acknowledged "performance limitations at scale that do not meet the capabilities described during the sales process" only on **January 12, 2023**, and remedial efforts continued through the April 15, 2023 patch. A March 22, 2024 filing is within one year of the failed remediation, and ongoing-repair promises support estoppel. The one-year period should also not reach the fraud count.
4. **Fraudulent inducement (12(b)(6)/9(b)):** The Complaint pleads the *who* (Sousa, Baines), *when* (Jan. 10, 2022 email; Jan. 18 and Feb. 7, 2022 demos; Feb. 14, 2022 SOW Capabilities Document), *what* (quoted representations: "out of the box with zero custom configuration"; "15,000 concurrent... records with sub-second latency"), and scienter (the Subramanian admission). A boilerplate integration clause without disclaimer-of-reliance language does not bar fraud in the inducement under Georgia or Texas law.
5. **Damages caps (§§ 11.1, 11.2):** These are affirmative defenses, not Rule 12 grounds, and should not cap fraudulent-inducement damages as a matter of public policy.

Moderate-risk areas: the 12(b)(1) amount-in-controversy attack is weak for DataCore but we should shore up factual support for the $1.2M consequential damages figure; the Texas choice-of-law argument (§ 14.3) is the main threat to Count IV (Georgia UDTPA).

## III. Issue-by-Issue Analysis

### A. Rule 12(b)(1) — Subject Matter Jurisdiction (weak attack; easily defeated)

**DataCore's argument:** the damages figures ($1.2M consequential; $2.335M "actual"; $931K reliance) are "speculative and inflated," casting doubt on the amount in controversy under 28 U.S.C. § 1332.

**Opposition:**
- Diversity of citizenship is effectively undisputed: Georgia plaintiff; Delaware/Texas defendant (Whitford Decl. ¶¶ 4–5 confirm incorporation and principal place of business). Complete diversity is conceded.
- Amount in controversy is tested by the good-faith / legal-certainty standard; Pinnacle's facial claim of $2,335,000 + $931,000 + $1,200,000 + punitive damages vastly exceeds $75,000. Even DataCore's own liability-cap figure of $432,000 (§ 11.1) exceeds the threshold eight-fold.
- DataCore's attack is on the weight of the evidence, not jurisdiction; at most it supports jurisdictional discovery, not dismissal.
- **To-do:** support the Greenleaf Accounting Group quantification and the Harmon Ridge Capital lending-impact allegations with a CFO or auditor declaration to blunt the "speculation" framing.

### B. Rule 12(b)(2) — Personal Jurisdiction (DataCore's weakest ground)

**DataCore's argument (per TOC/Preliminary Statement):** under *Daimler* and *Goodyear*, DataCore is "at home" in Austin, Texas, and Georgia contacts are insufficient.

**Opposition:**
1. **Specific jurisdiction is clearly pleaded** (Compl. ¶ 16):
   - Permanent Atlanta office at 100 Techwood Drive NW staffed by ~14 employees servicing Southeast clients including Pinnacle (¶¶ 12, 16(a));
   - Sousa, VP of Sales–Southeast, based in Atlanta, made the material misrepresentations *from* Georgia to a Georgia plaintiff (¶¶ 2, 25, 28–29);
   - Sousa signed the MSA from the Atlanta office (¶ 44);
   - The platform was implemented for and serviced to Pinnacle in Georgia, and the injuries were suffered in Georgia.
   This is textbook purposeful availment under *Burger King* and squarely within Georgia's long-arm statute (O.C.G.A. § 9-10-91, tortious act in this state).
2. **Critical evidentiary gap:** the Whitford Declaration details Austin operations (¶¶ 4–7) but **never denies** the Atlanta office, the 14 Georgia employees, or Sousa's Georgia base. DataCore's own evidentiary submission therefore fails to rebut Pinnacle's prima facie jurisdictional allegations, which must be accepted as true and resolved in Pinnacle's favor at this stage.
3. **General jurisdiction** (¶ 17, pleaded in the alternative) is a stretch post-*Daimler* (a regional office does not make a corporation "essentially at home"). Lead with specific jurisdiction; do not let the court conflate the theories.
4. **Reply point:** remote delivery and out-of-state hosting (Whitford ¶¶ 7, 14) are irrelevant — the claims arise from sales conduct directed at Georgia from Georgia, not server location.
5. **To-do:** Nance declaration confirming Sousa's Atlanta office and the email signature block ("100 Techwood Drive NW, Atlanta, GA 30318"); request jurisdictional discovery in the alternative.

### C. Rule 12(b)(3) — Venue / Forum Selection Clause

**DataCore's argument:** MSA § 14.2 mandates exclusive resolution in Travis County, Texas; clauses are entitled to substantial deference under the *Bremen* line.

**Opposition:**
1. **Wrong procedural vehicle.** Under *Atlantic Marine Constr. Co. v. U.S. Dist. Court* (511 U.S. 973 analogy not needed — 571 U.S. 49 (2013)), which DataCore's authorities conspicuously omit (it cites only *Bremen*/*Scherk*/*Stewart*), a forum-selection clause pointing to another federal forum is enforced through a **§ 1404(a) motion to transfer, not Rule 12(b)(3) dismissal**. Venue is facially proper in N.D. Ga. under § 1391(b)(2): the misrepresentations were made in this District by Georgia-based personnel; the MSA was negotiated, executed, and performed here; the injuries occurred here (Compl. ¶ 18). The 12(b)(3) motion should be denied on this ground alone.
2. **Scope of the clause.** § 14.2 covers disputes "arising out of or relating to this Agreement." Counts II–IV challenge **pre-contractual representations that induced execution** — a dispute over the formation of the Agreement itself, which the clause arguably does not reach, particularly because the SOW Capabilities Document was never incorporated into the MSA (Compl. ¶ 39).
3. **No fraud carve-in.** The clause contains no language stating that it survives a finding that the Agreement was induced by fraud. Enforcement against fraud-based claims is contested on that basis.
4. **Public-interest factors** under § 1404(a) favor Georgia: a Georgia plaintiff, Georgia witnesses, and Georgia's interest in its UDTPA and deceptive-conduct occurring in Georgia. Be candid that under *Atlantic Marine* private-interest factors are deemed to favor the contracted forum and public factors rarely defeat transfer — so the lead arguments are (1)–(3), with public interest as support.
5. **Even if transfer is granted,** dismissal is unwarranted; preserve all substantive arguments for the transferee court.

### D. Rule 12(b)(6) — Count I: Breach of Contract

**DataCore's arguments:** (1) time-barred by the § 9.4 one-year contractual limitations period because Pinnacle "discovered the issues... no later than August 2022" but sued in March 2024; (2) barred by the § 11.1 cap and § 11.2 consequential-damages waiver.

**Opposition:**
1. **Accrual is a fact question.** § 9.4 runs from when the claimant "knew or should have known of the facts giving rise to such claim." Timeline: August 2022 (ADP concerns) → November 2022 (latency at ~4,000 records) → December 5, 2022 formal notice → **January 12, 2023** Subramanian email acknowledging "performance limitations at scale that do not meet the capabilities described during the sales process" → **April 15, 2023** remedial patch (achieving only ~7,500 of the represented 15,000 records). The January 2023 admission shows that even DataCore had not conceded the issues were incurable until at least then; continuing remediation supports continuing-accrual and estoppel theories (DataCore's promised patch induced delay). Suit filed March 22, 2024 is within one year of the failed April 2023 remediation.
2. **Early "concerns" ≠ accrual of the breach.** Raising integration and latency concerns while the vendor promised fixes is not knowledge that the platform could never perform; Pinnacle is entitled to this inference on Rule 12.
3. **§§ 11.1/11.2 are affirmative defenses,** not pleading defects. At most they limit remedies after proof; their enforceability is fact-bound and unsuitable for Rule 12.
4. **Exploit the internal tension:** Whitford ¶ 12 states the forum-selection, choice-of-law, limitations, and liability-cap terms "were presented to Pinnacle in DataCore's initial proposal and were not modified during the negotiation process," while the Motion's Preliminary Statement calls the MSA "negotiated" after "months of arm's-length negotiations" with Pinnacle "represented by competent counsel throughout." Use this contradiction to attack the "freely negotiated" framing that underpins DataCore's *Bremen*, limitations, and cap arguments.

### E. Rule 12(b)(6) — Count II: Fraudulent Inducement

**DataCore's arguments:** (1) failure to plead fraud with particularity and plausibility (generalized product dissatisfaction recast as fraud); (2) the § 14.7 integration clause forecloses reliance.

**Opposition:**
1. **Rule 9(b) is satisfied.** The Complaint pleads:
   - **Who:** Sousa (VP Sales, Southeast) and Baines (sales engineer).
   - **When/where/how:** the January 10, 2022 Sousa email (quoted verbatim: "TalentBridge fully integrates with all major payroll platforms, including Paychex, ADP, and Gusto, out of the box with zero custom configuration. Your team won't need to spend a single hour on custom API work — it just works."); the January 18 and February 7, 2022 live demos using an allegedly specially configured staging environment (¶ 34); the February 14, 2022 SOW Capabilities Document ("up to 15,000 concurrent temporary worker records with sub-second latency"); the November 2021 marketing brochure ("native integration with 40+ payroll and HRIS platforms").
   - **Falsity:** ~$85,000 of undisclosed custom API work required; actual capacity ~4,000 concurrent records (a ~73% shortfall), and only ~7,500 even after the patch.
   - **Scienter:** the Subramanian January 12, 2023 admission of a gap between sales representations and actual capability ("performance limitations at scale that do not meet the capabilities described during the sales process").
   - **Justifiable reliance and materiality:** the two requirements were non-negotiable and communicated repeatedly (¶ 24); Pinnacle selected TalentBridge over two competing vendors (¶ 30).
2. **Integration clause does not bar fraudulent inducement.** Under both Georgia and Texas law, a general integration clause does not negate reliance absent clear, specific disclaimer-of-reliance (anti-reliance) language, which § 14.7 lacks. It is a standard merger clause.
3. **The "sophisticated parties represented by counsel" framing** is a fact argument, not a pleading bar — and Whitford's ¶¶ 12–13 confirmation that the terms were DataCore's unmodified standard form cuts against treating them as individually negotiated.

### F. Rule 12(b)(6) — Count III: Negligent Misrepresentation

**DataCore's arguments:** integration clause; economic loss rule.

**Opposition:**
1. **Fraud/inducement exception to the economic loss rule:** where misrepresentations induced entry into the contract, tort recovery is available because the duty not to deceive is independent of the contract (Georgia's *Robert & Co. v. Rhodes-Haverty* line; Texas's *Formosa Plastics Corp. v. Presidio Engineers*). This is the classic carve-out.
2. **Independent duty:** the duty not to make knowingly false capability representations to induce a purchase is independent of the MSA's performance obligations.
3. **Risk assessment:** this count, together with Count IV, is the most exposed. Oppose on *Formosa*-style grounds, but prioritize briefing resources on Counts I–II.

### G. Rule 12(b)(6) — Count IV: Georgia UDTPA (O.C.G.A. § 10-1-370 et seq.)

**DataCore's arguments:** duplicative of the contract claim; barred by the economic loss rule; displaced by the § 14.3 Texas choice-of-law clause.

**Opposition:**
1. **Choice of law is contested.** § 14.3 governs "this Agreement" and disputes "arising under this Agreement." A statutory claim predicated on pre-contractual deceptive conduct committed in Georgia by Georgia-based employees arguably does not "arise under" the Agreement; Georgia choice-of-law rules (lex loci delicti) point to Georgia for tort/statutory claims. This is our weakest count — the scope-of-choice-of-law-clause fight is genuinely uncertain.
2. **Alternative pleading:** if the court applies Texas law, request leave to amend to plead the Texas DTPA (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 17.41 et seq.). DataCore's own brief cites Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 2.725 in its limitations argument, effectively conceding the court must choose between statutory regimes.
3. **Not duplicative:** a statutory claim resting on pre-contractual misrepresentations is distinct from breach of contractual obligations.
4. **Caveat:** Georgia's UDTPA primarily affords injunctive relief; the remedy theory in Count IV sits in the truncated portion of the Complaint — verify and be prepared for a standing/remedy attack not visible in the reviewed text.

### H. Cross-Cutting: Limitations, Caps, and Choice of Law Across All Counts

- **§ 9.4:** even if enforceable as to contract claims, it should not shorten the fraud limitations period; enforceability under Texas and Georgia public policy is contestable (§ 2.725 permits contractual reduction to one year in UCC-goods contexts; this is a SaaS/services contract — brief carefully).
- **§§ 11.1/11.2 caps:** fraud-based damages typically escape contractual caps and consequential-damages waivers as a matter of public policy; do not concede application to Counts II–IV.
- **Unconscionability/adhesion backup:** Whitford ¶¶ 12–13 hand us the record — standard-form terms, "one of approximately 1,400" identical agreements, unmodified terms — supporting procedural-unconscionability arguments against the forum, limitations, and cap clauses.

## IV. Strategic Recommendations

1. **Lead with 12(b)(2) and the 12(b)(3) procedural defect** (*Atlantic Marine*: a forum-selection clause does not make venue "improper"). These are the cleanest wins.
2. **Attack the Whitford Declaration's omissions** — no denial of the Atlanta office or Sousa's Georgia presence — with a Nance declaration attaching the Sousa emails (signature block showing 100 Techwood Drive).
3. **Build the opposition's timeline** around the January 12, 2023 Subramanian admission and the April 15, 2023 patch to defeat the § 9.4 argument as a fact question, with estoppel from promised remediation.
4. **Exploit the "negotiated vs. standard form" contradiction** between the Motion's Preliminary Statement and Whitford ¶ 12.
5. **Damage control on 12(b)(1):** a Greenleaf declaration supporting the $1.2M consequential figure; the attack is weak but cheap to neutralize.
6. **Preserve amendment:** in the alternative to dismissal of any count, request leave to amend (especially Count IV → Texas DTPA if Georgia law is displaced).
7. **Evidence to obtain and verify:** the original January 10, 2022 Sousa email and February 14, 2022 SOW email; the January 12, 2023 Subramanian email; the November 2021 marketing brochure; ADP custom-development invoices (~$85,000); load-test documentation (4,000 / 7,500 / 15,000 record figures); Harmon Ridge Capital correspondence; the December 5, 2022 notice letter; and the January 15, 2024 termination notice. ## V. Conclusion

The motion is procedurally defective on venue (Rule 12(b)(3) is the wrong vehicle), factually unrebuted on personal jurisdiction, and premature on every 12(b)(6) ground, each of which turns on fact questions (accrual, scope of the one-year period, applicability of the caps, and effect of a boilerplate integration clause) inappropriate for Rule 12 resolution. Recommend filing a combined opposition supported by Nance and auditor declarations, with an alternative request for jurisdictional discovery and leave to amend.
