## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Tidewater raises six objections: (1) the sale cannot proceed free and clear of its franchise interest under § 363(f); (2) the $4.755 million break-up fee is excessive and chilled bidding; (3) Grandview is an insider due to the Haines–Townsend relationship; (4) exclusion of the Terminated Franchise Agreements deprives Tidewater of § 365 cure rights; (5) Tidewater should be allowed to submit a late $160 million bid; and (6) ¶ 18 of the proposed Sale Order contains an impermissible non-consensual release.

The first, second, fourth, and fifth objections are weak and largely answerable from the record — most decisively, the APA expressly **excludes** the Terminated Franchise Agreements from the Purchased Assets (APA § 2.2(d); APA Summary § IV), which moots the § 363(f) and § 365 cure arguments, and Tidewater's proposed $160 million bid is facially below the court-approved minimum qualified bid of **$164,255,000** (Bid Procedures Order ¶ 5(a)) and untimely under the July 15, 2024 Bid Deadline (¶ 6). The third objection (insider connection) will draw the most judicial attention and requires evidentiary support (Medina declaration; Langford Rae process record; UCC investigation). The sixth objection (¶ 18 release) is the only one where we should consider a **conforming language change** to the Sale Order rather than pure opposition, because the release text ("any and all claims ... arising out of or related to the Sale Transaction") is broad enough to arguably reach prepetition claims.

A separate, material risk outside the sale hearing: the MFA excerpts suggest Tidewater's cure payment on **October 20, 2023 — 28 days after the September 22, 2023 Default Notice — was within the 45-day Cure Period under MFA § 12.3**, and the November 15, 2023 Termination Letter made termination "effective immediately," contrary to MFA § 12.4 (termination effective not less than 10 and not more than 30 days after the Termination Notice). The Debtor's eventual claim objection is vulnerable; this should inform settlement posture but should not affect sale approval. A possible Debtor counter on the claim: § 12.3 requires cure of Payment Defaults to include "accrued interest as provided in Section 7.1" (1.5%/month), so the $1,185,000 wire may have been incomplete — a fact to develop.

## II. ARGUMENT-BY-ARGUMENT ANALYSIS AND RESPONSE STRATEGY

### Objection 1 — Sale cannot proceed free and clear of Tidewater's franchise interest under 11 U.S.C. § 363(f)

**Tidewater's position:** It does not consent; its franchise rights are an "interest in property" under § 363(f) (citing *Folger Adam*, *TWA*); none of the five § 363(f) conditions is satisfied, so the sale cannot extinguish its franchise interests.

**Assessment: Weak — primarily moot.**

**Responses:**
1. **The objection attacks a sale provision that does not exist.** Section 2.2(d) of the APA expressly excludes the four Terminated Franchise Agreements from the Purchased Assets (APA Summary § IV(d); Sale Motion ¶ 22). Section 363(f) strips interests only from assets **being sold**. Because the MFA and related agreements are not conveyed, the free-and-clear provision of the Sale Order cannot operate on Tidewater's franchise interests even if valid. The APA Summary (§ IV) states this expressly: "the proposed sale free and clear under Section 363(f) ... is not implicated with respect to Tidewater's alleged franchise relationship because that relationship ... is simply not part of the transaction."
2. **Even if § 363(f) were implicated, at least two conditions are satisfied.** (a) § 363(f)(4): the existence, validity, and effect of Tidewater's alleged franchise interest is squarely in **bona fide dispute** — the Debtor terminated the MFA pre-petition on November 15, 2023 and maintains termination was valid (Sale Motion ¶¶ 20–21); Tidewater disputes it. Tidewater's own objection concedes "the validity of the MFA termination may be in dispute," undermining its categorical assertion that no condition applies. (b) § 363(f)(5): Tidewater itself reduced its franchise grievance to a **money claim** — $6.8 million in Proof of Claim No. 247 — demonstrating it can be compelled to accept money satisfaction. Its claim for "lost franchise fee savings" and "lost profits" is quintessentially compensable in damages.
3. **The MFA itself contemplated free transferability by the franchisor.** MFA § 14.7 permits Franchisor to "freely assign th[e] Agreement ... to any affiliate or successor in connection with a sale of all or substantially all of Franchisor's assets" without franchisee consent — undercutting Tidewater's § 363(f)(1) position (the parties' own contract, i.e., applicable nonbankruptcy law, permits the transfer).
4. **Tidewater's rights are fully preserved.** The MFA/Tidewater dispute remains with the estate and will be resolved in the claims process (Sale Motion ¶ 22; APA Summary § IV).

**Evidentiary needs:** APA § 2.2 and Excluded Assets schedule; Proof of Claim No. 247; MFA §§ 12.3, 14.7.

### Objection 2 — The $4.755 million break-up fee is excessive and chilled bidding

**Tidewater's position:** 3.0% break-up fee (aggregate protections $6.255 million / ~3.95%) is excessive; the absence of qualified bids proves chilling.

**Assessment: Weak — waived and merits-barred.**

**Responses:**
1. **The fee was already approved by final Court order after notice and hearing, with no objection from Tidewater.** The Bid Procedures Order (entered May 20, 2024) approved the Break-Up Fee (¶ 12), the Expense Reimbursement cap of $1.5 million (¶ 13), and made an express finding that the aggregate protections (~3.95%) "are reasonable and appropriate ... and will not unduly chill competitive bidding" and are "within the range of protections approved by courts in this District" (¶ 14). The Court also found the fee to be an actual and necessary cost under § 503(b)(1)(A) and consistent with market practice (¶ 12). The record shows **no party objected** to the Bid Procedures Motion (BPO Finding F). The Order's objection-waiver provisions bar this late attack (BPO ¶ 16, absent cause shown).
2. **The fee is within market norms.** 3.0% break-up fee / ~3.95% aggregate protections is consistent with customary 363-sale ranges; the Court has already so found.
3. **No chilling.** The marketing process was extensive: 85+ parties contacted, 42 NDAs/CIMs, 18 data-room participants, 7 indications of interest, multiple site visits (Sale Motion ¶¶ 24, 26). Non-bidding parties cited portfolio capex needs, market conditions, or the DIP timeline — not the fee (Sale Motion ¶ 32). No bid ever exceeded $158.5 million (¶ 32).
4. **Law-of-the-case/collateral attack framing:** Tidewater's real quarrel is with the Bid Procedures Order; the proper vehicle was a timely bid-procedures objection or appeal, not a sale objection.

**Note (record hygiene):** The Sale Motion, UCC statement, and Tidewater objection cite inconsistent docket numbers for the Bid Procedures Order (Dkt. 142 / Dkt. 148 / Dkt. 156) and inconsistent marketing statistics (Sale Motion: 85 contacted / 42 NDAs / 18 interested / 7 IOIs vs. UCC: 120 / 23 / 5). Be prepared to reconcile these on the record; the discrepancies are harmless but Tidewater could exploit them on credibility.

### Objection 3 — Grandview is an insider; heightened scrutiny required

**Tidewater's position:** Gerald Haines (Grandview MD, lead deal professional) is married to Catherine Haines née Townsend, sister of Debtor CEO Marcus Townsend. Disclosure and recusal are inadequate: recusal is self-reported; Medina reports to Townsend; Langford Rae was engaged under Townsend's leadership before the recusal; no competing bids suggests a process structured to favor Grandview.

**Assessment: Tidewater's strongest objection. Defensible, but requires affirmative evidentiary proof.**

**Responses:**
1. **Full and timely disclosure.** The relationship was disclosed in ¶ 47 of the Sale Motion and ¶ 23 of the Bid Procedures Motion (Tidewater's objection concedes this, ¶ 28). There was no concealment.
2. **Recusal and process separation.** Townsend recused from all Grandview negotiations and from the May 30, 2024 board deliberation and unanimous vote authorizing the APA (Sale Motion ¶¶ 28–29). COO Patricia Medina — board-designated — led the sale process (Sale Motion ¶¶ 25, 28).
3. **Independent checks.** Langford Rae, a nationally recognized, court-approved hospitality investment banker (retention approved April 8, 2024 with UCC support, Sale Motion ¶ 23), ran the marketing process; the UCC had full data-room access, received all IOIs and bid letters, attended key negotiation sessions, and consulted on APA terms before execution (Sale Motion ¶ 31). The UCC then conducted its own independent investigation and expressly concluded the process was "fair, open, transparent, and conducted at arm's length" and that the safeguards (complete recusal, independent banker, full disclosure) were adequate (UCC Statement §§ I, III.A). Notably, the UCC states Langford Rae had **no prior relationship** with Grandview or Haines — a direct rebuttal to Tidewater's engagement-timing insinuation.
4. **"Insider" status is overstated.** Grandview is not a statutory insider of the Debtor; the connection is one degree removed (spouse of the CEO's sister). Heightened scrutiny of process and price is appropriate, but the record — extensive marketing, UCC oversight, court-approved procedures, no overbid — supports the conclusion that the process was not tainted and the price was the highest obtained (Sale Motion ¶¶ 26, 30, 32). Consider offering a Langford Rae fairness/valuation declaration.
5. **Anticipate the DIP-timeline theme** (Objection ¶ 9): answer with the severity of default consequences under the DIP Facility — acceleration, termination of cash collateral use, possible Chapter 7 conversion (Sale Motion ¶ 17; UCC Statement § II.B) — and the court-approved nature of the timeline.

**Gaps to close before the hearing:** (a) a Medina declaration detailing her negotiation role and board designation; (b) confirmation of the mechanics and timing of Townsend's recusal (board minutes; any email record — note insider-disclosure-email.eml could not be read and may be relevant here); (c) Langford Rae declaration on engagement timing and absence of prior Grandview/Haines relationship.

### Objection 4 — APA's exclusion of the Terminated Franchise Agreements deprives Tidewater of § 365 cure rights

**Tidewater's position:** Excluding the MFA from the Purchased Assets circumvents Tidewater's rights as a counterparty to an executory contract and its § 365 cure rights.

**Assessment: Weak — internally contradictory.**

**Responses:**
1. **Tidewater cannot have it both ways.** Objection 1 attacks the sale *for including* its franchise interest; Objection 4 attacks the sale *for excluding* it. The exclusion is precisely what protects Tidewater: nothing is transferred, assumed, or stripped.
2. **§ 365 is not implicated.** Cure rights arise when a debtor **assumes or rejects** an executory contract. The MFA was terminated pre-petition (November 15, 2023), is not on the Assumed Contracts Schedule (APA Summary § V: the schedule "does not include the MFA or any agreements with Tidewater"), and the estate's exposure to Tidewater — if any — is a prepetition damages claim (Claim No. 247) to be resolved in claims administration (Sale Motion ¶¶ 21–22). All liabilities "arising from or related to the Terminated Franchise Agreements, the MFA, or the Tidewater dispute" are Excluded Liabilities retained by the estate (APA Summary § V).
3. **The sale does not diminish Tidewater's recovery:** its claim, whatever its allowance, rides through the sale unaffected; Tidewater remains free to litigate Claim No. 247 (and the Debtor remains free to object to it — Sale Motion ¶ 21).

### Objection 5 — Tidewater should be permitted to submit a late $160.0 million competing bid

**Tidewater's position:** A $160 million late bid exceeds the stalking horse bid and would maximize value.

**Assessment: Very weak — facially deficient on multiple independent grounds.**

**Responses:**
1. **The bid is below the minimum Qualified Bid.** The court-approved minimum is **$164,255,000** ($158,500,000 + $4,755,000 Break-Up Fee + $1,000,000 initial overbid increment) (BPO ¶ 5(a)). At $160 million, Tidewater's bid is deficient by more than $4.2 million (UCC Statement § V). On its face it does not "exceed" the stalking horse bid once the fee is accounted for.
2. **It is late.** The Bid Deadline was July 15, 2024, 5:00 p.m. ET, "time is of the essence"; late bids "shall not be considered ... for any purpose ... unless the Court, upon motion and for cause shown, orders otherwise" (BPO ¶ 6). Tidewater made no motion for cause and identified no diligence or financing impediment — it was a franchise counterparty with full access to the process throughout.
3. **No deposit, no financing, no mark-up.** Qualified Bids require a $15,850,000 cash deposit (10%), an executed mark-up of the APA, committed financing or equivalent proof of capability, no contingencies, and irrevocability (BPO ¶ 5(b)–(f)). Nothing in the objection suggests Tidewater (a four-hotel operator whose claimed annual brand-attributable profit is ~$851k per its own proof of claim) could satisfy these requirements; the deposit alone exceeds half of its asserted $6.8 million claim.
4. **No "cause" and severe prejudice.** Reopening bidding now would jeopardize the September 30, 2024 DIP Maturity Milestone (Sale Motion ¶ 17; BPO Finding C), risking DIP default, acceleration, loss of cash-collateral use, and potential Chapter 7 conversion (Sale Motion ¶ 17; UCC Statement §§ II.B, IV) — catastrophic to all unsecured creditors, including Tidewater.
5. **Strategic character of the bid:** Tidewater's posture (wrongful-termination claimant seeking to leverage the sale) supports denying equitable relief.

### Objection 6 — ¶ 18 of the proposed Sale Order contains an impermissible non-consensual release

**Tidewater's position:** ¶ 18 releases the Buyer, Seller, and their officers/directors/agents/employees from "any and all claims, causes of action, or liabilities arising out of or related to the Sale Transaction," impermissibly extinguishing Tidewater's prepetition claims including the $6.8 million Claim No. 247.

**Assessment: Defensible on construction, but the cheapest fix is a clarifying carve-out. Recommend a language change rather than a fight.**

**Responses:**
1. **The release is transactional, not global.** As quoted in Tidewater's own objection (¶ 23), ¶ 18 releases claims "arising out of or related to the **Sale Transaction**." Tidewater's Claim No. 247 arises from the November 15, 2023 pre-petition termination of the MFA — not from the 2024 sale. Read properly, the release does not reach Claim No. 247.
2. **The APA's structure confirms the point:** the MFA and all Tidewater-related liabilities are Excluded Assets/Excluded Liabilities retained by the estate (APA Summary §§ IV(d), V); the Sale Motion states the exclusion "ensures that the Sale does not affect Tidewater's ability to assert claims against the estate through the claims allowance process" (Sale Motion ¶ 22).
3. **Non-consensual third-party releases are disfavored** in the Fourth Circuit and elsewhere, and a 363 sale order — unlike a plan — cannot discharge estate liabilities or release creditors' claims against the estate over objection.
4. **Recommended strategy:** conform ¶ 18 to add express limiting language, e.g., "For the avoidance of doubt, this release (i) applies only to claims arising from or relating to the negotiation, documentation, and consummation of the Sale Transaction, (ii) does not release or extinguish any pre-petition claim against the estate, including Proof of Claim No. 247, which shall be resolved in the claims allowance process, and (iii) effects no release of or by any non-consenting third party." This moots the objection at trivial cost and removes Tidewater's only facially sympathetic hook. Confirm whether the UCC's negotiated Sale Order modifications (referenced in Tidewater's objection ¶ 10) already address this.

## III. CONSOLIDATED HEARING STRATEGY

1. **Lead with mootness of Objections 1 and 4** (APA § 2.2(d) exclusion) — it reframes Tidewater as seeking leverage, not protection.
2. **Assert waiver/collateral attack** as to Objection 2 (bid protections finally litigated in the Bid Procedures Order with no Tidewater participation).
3. **Affirmatively prove the process** on Objection 3 with declarations (Medina, Langford Rae), board minutes, and the UCC's independent investigation; concede heightened scrutiny and meet it.
4. **Quantify the deficiencies** of the $160 million bid (below the $164,255,000 threshold; late; no deposit/financing/mark-up) and the DIP Milestone prejudice of reopening bidding.
5. **Offer the ¶ 18 carve-out** to moot Objection 6.
6. **Fallback relief:** if the Court is inclined to entertain any Tidewater bid, request conditions: full Qualified Bid compliance (deposit, committed financing, APA mark-up, irrevocability), minimum price of $164,255,000, a closing schedule compliant with the DIP Milestone, and Grandview's Break-Up Fee/Expense Reimbursement paid or escrowed.
7. **Reconcile record discrepancies** (BPO docket numbers; marketing statistics) before Tidewater raises them on cross or argument.

## IV. SEPARATE RISK NOTE — UNDERLYING CLAIM LITIGATION EXPOSURE

Independent of the sale hearing, the documents read together create real exposure on Claim No. 247 that should inform settlement posture:
- **The cure appears timely.** MFA § 12.3 provides a 45-day Cure Period from the Default Notice; Tidewater wired $1,185,000 on October 20, 2023 — day 28 — and holds a wire confirmation (Proof of Claim § 3, Supporting Doc. 2). The Sale Motion's assertion that termination followed "expiration of the applicable cure period" (¶ 20) is difficult to square with § 12.3.
- **The termination's immediate effectiveness may be procedurally defective.** MFA § 12.4 requires a Termination Date "not less than ten (10) days and not more than thirty (30) days after" the Termination Notice; the November 15, 2023 letter was "effective immediately" (Proof of Claim § 3; Objection ¶ 16).
- **Possible Debtor counters:** (a) cure of a Payment Default under § 12.3 requires payment in full "together with any accrued interest as provided in Section 7.1" (1.5%/month), so the flat $1,185,000 wire may not have been a complete cure; (b) damages methodology — the "lost franchise fee savings" component ($2.97M) is conceptually incoherent (fees Tidewater would have *paid* are a cost, not a damage, and appear to double-count with the lost-profits component, which should already be net of fees); (c) the lost-profits figure rests on estimated "incremental brand value" (~$851,111/year), with expert supplementation merely reserved.
