## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Tidewater Lodging Ventures, LLC ("Tidewater"), a disputed general unsecured creditor with a $6.8 million claim (Proof of Claim No. 247), raises six objections to the proposed $158.5 million sale to Grandview Capital Partners, LLC. Our assessment:

| # | Objection | Strength | Recommended Response |
|---|-----------|----------|----------------------|
| 1 | § 363(f) cannot strip Tidewater's franchise interest | **Weak** | Terminated Franchise Agreements are Excluded Assets under APA § 2.2(d); § 363(f) not implicated |
| 2 | Break-up fee excessive / chilled bidding | **Weak** | Court-approved in Bid Procedures Order ¶¶ 12–14; law of the case; no timely objection then |
| 3 | Grandview is an insider; heightened scrutiny | **Moderate — the only objection requiring real care** | Full disclosure, recusal, board vote without Townsend, independent banker, UCC investigation and support |
| 4 | Exclusion of Terminated Franchise Agreements deprives Tidewater of § 365 cure rights | **Weak / internally contradictory** | MFA was terminated pre-petition; nothing to assume or cure; exclusion *protects* Tidewater's claims process |
| 5 | Tidewater should be allowed a late $160.0 million bid | **Very weak** | Below $164,255,000 minimum qualified bid; late (bid deadline July 15); no deposit or financing showing; jeopardizes DIP milestone |
| 6 | ¶ 18 of Sale Order is an impermissible non-consensual release | **Moderate** | Narrow the release to claims "arising out of or relating to the Sale Transaction" with explicit carve-out preserving Proof of Claim No. 247 — cheap concession |

Bottom line: The objection is unlikely to defeat the sale. The two arguments requiring affirmative preparation are the insider-relationship argument (Objection 3) and the release language (Objection 6). We recommend offering tailored Sale Order language on the release and evidentiary support (Medina declaration, Langford Rae testimony) on process integrity, and otherwise relying on the Bid Procedures Order and the APA's Excluded Assets structure.

## II. BACKGROUND

- **The case:** Pinnacle (37 hotels, ~2,800 employees, FY2023 revenue ~$189.4M) filed Chapter 11 on March 18, 2024, after defaulting on the Waverly prepetition facility (~$171.2M outstanding against $175M commitment). Scheduled assets ~$214.7M vs. liabilities ~$267.3M (Sale Motion ¶¶ 8–13).
- **DIP financing:** $22M from Waverly with a September 30, 2024 sale-consummation milestone; default risks acceleration, loss of cash collateral use, and possible conversion to Chapter 7 (Sale Motion ¶ 17; UCC Statement § II.B).
- **The sale:** APA dated June 1, 2024 with Grandview; $158.5M total ($141M cash + $17.5M assumed liabilities: $11.2M cure costs + $6.3M trade payables). Stalking horse protections: $4.755M break-up fee (3.0%) plus $1.5M expense cap. Bid Procedures Order entered May 20, 2024: bid deadline July 15, 2024; minimum qualified bid $164,255,000; 10% deposit ($15.85M); auction July 22; sale hearing July 29; no competing qualified bids were received.
- **The Tidewater dispute:** MFA dated June 1, 2018 (10-year term to May 31, 2028); fees of 5.5% royalty, 2.0% marketing, $150K/hotel technology fee (~$2.97M/yr on ~$31.6M gross room revenue). Default notice September 22, 2023 (~$1.185M unpaid Q2/Q3 royalties and marketing); Tidewater wired $1.185M on October 20, 2023 (day 28); Pinnacle terminated November 15, 2023. Tidewater filed a $6.8M proof of claim on May 1, 2024. Critically, **MFA § 12.3 provides a 45-day cure period**, and § 12.4 requires a termination notice with an effective date 10–30 days after the notice — both potentially in tension with Pinnacle's "effective immediately" termination. However, note the internal tension in Tidewater's own filing: its proof of claim describes the Termination Notice as "terminated effective immediately," while MFA § 12.4 required at least 10 days' notice — a merits issue for claim objection, not for the sale.
- **Insider relationship:** Gerald Haines, Grandview Managing Director leading the acquisition, is married to Catherine Haines (née Townsend), sister of Pinnacle CEO Marcus Townsend. Disclosed in Sale Motion ¶ 47 and Bid Procedures Motion ¶ 23; Townsend recused; COO Patricia Medina led negotiations; the board (minus Townsend) unanimously approved the APA on May 30, 2024 (Sale Motion ¶ 29).
- **UCC position:** Full support after independent investigation (data room access, review of marketing, banker consultations); the UCC filed a statement specifically rebutting Tidewater's objection.

## III. ARGUMENT-BY-ARGUMENT ANALYSIS AND RESPONSE STRATEGY

### Objection 1 — Sale Cannot Proceed Free and Clear of Tidewater's Franchise Interest Under § 363(f)

**Tidewater's argument:** Tidewater has not consented; none of the five § 363(f) conditions is satisfied as to its franchise interest (citing *In re Trans World Airlines*, 322 F.3d 283 (3d Cir. 2003); *Folger Adam Sec., Inc. v. DeMatteis/MacGregor JV*, 209 F.3d 252 (3d Cir. 2000)).

**Analysis:** This is Tidewater's flagship argument, but it is structurally flawed. Section 363(f) strips interests only from property **being sold**. Under APA § 2.2(d) (APA Summary § IV(d)), the four Terminated Franchise Agreements are **Excluded Assets**: they are neither transferred to Grandview nor assumed by him, and all liabilities relating to the Tidewater dispute are Excluded Liabilities (APA Summary § V). As the APA Summary itself states, § 363(f) "is not implicated" because the Tidewater relationship "is simply not part of the transaction." Whether the November 15, 2023 termination was valid (and hence whether Tidewater holds an unexpired executory contract or merely a damages claim) is irrelevant to the sale and will be resolved in claims administration — the Debtor has already stated it will object to Proof of Claim No. 247 (Sale Motion ¶ 21).

**Response strategy:**
1. Lead with the Excluded Assets structure: the sale does not purport to transfer or extinguish any Tidewater franchise interest; § 363(f) never operates on it.
2. Argue in the alternative that Tidewater's interest, if any, is at minimum **in bona fide dispute** under § 363(f)(4) — the termination dispute is genuine (Tidewater claims timely cure on day 28 of a 45-day period; the Debtor asserts the cure was untimely), which alone authorizes a free-and-clear sale as to disputed interests.
3. Emphasize that Tidewater's real remedy — allowance of its $6.8M claim — is fully preserved; liens/claims attach to proceeds under the APA structure.
4. Consider a belt-and-suspenders Sale Order clarification that nothing in the order adjudicates the validity of the MFA termination or Proof of Claim No. 247.

**Risk:** Low. But if the Court were to find the MFA still in effect, Tidewater would hold an unexpired executory contract — see Objection 4.

### Objection 2 — Break-Up Fee Excessive and Chilled Bidding

**Tidewater's argument:** $4.755M (3.0%) break-up fee (with $1.5M expense reimbursement, ~3.95% aggregate) is excessive and chilled bidding, evidenced by zero competing bids.

**Analysis:** Weak. The Break-Up Fee and Expense Reimbursement were **approved by this Court on May 20, 2024** after notice and a May 17 hearing at which **no party — including Tidewater — objected** (Bid Procedures Order ¶¶ F, 12–14). The Court made express findings that the fee is reasonable, market-consistent, and "will not unduly chill competitive bidding" (¶ 14). The aggregate protections (~3.95%) are within market norms (3–4% plus expenses). The absence of competing bids is equally attributable to portfolio capex needs and market softness — the Sale Motion itself (¶ 32) records that declining bidders cited capex, competitive landscape, and DIP timing.

**Response strategy:**
1. Law of the case / waiver: Bid Procedures Order is a final, unappealed order entered without objection; Tidewater's challenge is an untimely collateral attack.
2. Rebut causation: 85+ parties contacted, 42 NDAs, 18 data-room participants (Sale Motion ¶ 24); non-bidders cited business reasons, not the fee. (Note the UCC Statement cites different marketing figures — 120 contacted, 23 NDAs, 5 IOIs — vs. the Sale Motion's 85/42/18; **reconcile these numbers with Langford Rae before the hearing**, as Tidewater could exploit the discrepancy to attack process credibility.)
3. The fee is payable **only** upon an alternative sale — it costs the estate nothing if Grandview buys.

### Objection 3 — Grandview Is an Insider; Heightened Scrutiny

**Tidewater's argument:** Haines (Grandview lead) is CEO Townsend's brother-in-law; disclosure and recusal are inadequate; Medina reports to Townsend; Langford Rae was selected before recusal. Demands heightened scrutiny / possible entire-fairness-type review.

**Analysis:** This is Tidewater's most substantive argument and the only one with genuine evidentiary traction. Grandview is not a statutory insider of the Debtor (§ 101(31)) — Haines is not a debtor insider, and Townsend has no Grandview role disclosed — but the non-statutory insider / heightened-scrutiny doctrine for sales to insiders or connected parties is a real doctrine, and a brother-in-law link between buyer's lead dealmaker and seller's CEO will get the Court's attention. That said, the record of safeguards is strong:
- Full, voluntary disclosure in both the Bid Procedures Motion (¶ 23) and Sale Motion (¶ 47);
- Townsend's complete recusal from negotiation and the unanimous board vote (Sale Motion ¶ 29 — all directors voting except Townsend);
- COO Medina led negotiations;
- Langford Rae, an independent banker with no prior Grandview/Haines relationship (UCC Statement § III.A), ran the marketing;
- The UCC — with data room access, review of all IOIs and bid letters, and attendance at key negotiation sessions — independently investigated and affirmatively supports the sale;
- The transaction resulted from a broad market check with multiple IOIs (seven preliminary IOIs per Sale Motion ¶ 26), and Grandview's price was the highest.

Tidewater's counterpoints (Medina reports to Townsend; banker hired pre-recusal) are arguable but cannot overcome the combination of market check + independent fiduciary involvement + UCC vetting + court-approved procedures + the economic reality that Waverly ($171.2M secured, DIP lender) has every incentive to maximize proceeds.

**Response strategy:**
1. Concede the relationship; frame the issue as whether the *process* was arm's length, and marshal the safeguards above.
2. Prepare a **declaration of Patricia Medina** detailing the negotiation process, her reporting line during the process (consider board- or special-committee-style reporting), and Townsend's non-involvement.
3. Have **Langford Rae available to testify** at the hearing on the market check, IOI comparisons, and Grandview's pricing relative to other interest.
4. Highlight Waverly's consent to the sale and its $171.2M exposure — the party with the most to lose from a lowball sale supports the price.
5. If pressed, offer enhanced findings in the Sale Order reciting the court-approved process, disclosure, recusal, UCC investigation, and market check — courts approve insider-adjacent sales where process protections are robust.
6. Verify the substance of the insider-disclosure email (unreadable here) to confirm the disclosure timeline and content match the Sale Motion representations.

**Risk:** Moderate — could produce heightened scrutiny or additional findings, but denial is unlikely given the record.

### Objection 4 — Exclusion of Terminated Franchise Agreements Deprives Tidewater of § 365 Cure Rights

**Tidewater's argument:** APA § 2.2's exclusion of the Terminated Franchise Agreements improperly deprives Tidewater of cure rights under § 365.

**Analysis:** Internally contradictory and weak. Section 365 cure rights (§ 365(b)) belong to the **trustee/debtor** when assuming a contract — not to a counterparty, and not to compel assumption. The estate has no obligation to assume the MFA, and Tidewater cannot force assumption. Moreover, this argument contradicts Objection 1: Tidewater simultaneously argues (a) the sale strips its franchise interest and (b) the exclusion deprives it of rights. If the MFA was wrongfully terminated and remains executory, the Debtor may simply decline to assume it, leaving Tidewater with a § 365(g)/§ 502(g)-style rejection-damages claim — i.e., a general unsecured claim, which is exactly what Tidewater already asserts. If the termination was valid, there is no contract to assume. Either way, the sale structure (Excluded Asset; Excluded Liability) preserves Tidewater's claims-process remedies.

**Response strategy:**
1. Point out the contradiction with Objection 1.
2. State the black-letter rule: counterparties cannot compel assumption; § 365(b) cure obligations arise only upon the debtor's election to assume.
3. Reiterate that the exclusion affirmatively *protects* Tidewater — its claims remain with the estate for adjudication (APA Summary § IV(d); Sale Motion ¶ 22).

### Objection 5 — Tidewater Should Be Permitted a Late $160.0 Million Competing Bid

**Tidewater's argument:** A $160M late bid exceeds the stalking horse price and maximizes value.

**Analysis:** Very weak, and the UCC has already done much of the work (UCC Statement § V):
- **Below the minimum bid:** $160.0M < $164,255,000 (Bid Procedures Order ¶ 5(a): stalking horse price + break-up fee + $1M initial overbid). The bid is deficient by more than $4.2M — it is not even a value-over argument on its face, because topping Grandview would also trigger the $4.755M break-up fee.
- **Untimely:** Bid deadline was July 15, 2024, 5:00 p.m. ET, with express language that late bids "shall not be considered ... unless the Court, upon motion and for cause shown, orders otherwise" (¶ 6). No cause is shown: Tidewater was served with the Bid Procedures Order, filed no objection then, and articulates no reason it could not have bid on time.
- **No compliance showing:** No $15.85M deposit, no proof of committed financing, no APA markup, no irrevocability — every Qualified Bid requirement (¶ 5(b)–(f)) is unmet. There is no indication Tidewater (a four-hotel franchisee already claiming financial distress) has $160M of committed capital.
- **Timing/milestone prejudice:** Accepting a late bid would restart diligence and closing risk against the September 30 DIP Maturity Milestone; default risks acceleration and Chapter 7 conversion — a catastrophic outcome for unsecured creditors (UCC Statement §§ IV, V).

**Response strategy:**
1. Lead with the numbers (below minimum by $4.255M+; break-up fee makes the estate worse off than the Grandview deal at $160M).
2. Procedural default and waiver under Bid Procedures Order ¶ 6.
3. Demand proof of financial capability if the Court entertains the motion at all.
4. Lean on the UCC's opposition and the DIP milestone.

### Objection 6 — Paragraph 18 of the Sale Order Contains an Impermissible Non-Consensual Release

**Tidewater's argument:** The release ("Buyer, Seller, and their respective officers, directors, agents, and employees shall be released from any and all claims ... arising out of or related to the Sale Transaction") would extinguish Tidewater's $6.8M claim.

**Analysis:** This objection has legal force. Non-consensual third-party releases outside a plan are disfavored and, following *Purdue Pharma* (Harrington v. Purdue Pharma, 603 U.S. 204 (2024)), nonconsensual third-party releases of non-debtors are not authorized. Although ¶ 18 is arguably a narrow transaction-related release rather than a global release of estate claims, its "officers, directors, agents, and employees" language and "any and all claims ... related to the Sale Transaction" breadth could arguably be read to sweep in claims arising from the sale *process* (e.g., Tidewater's insider-process theories) and creates ambiguity that invites exactly this objection.

**Response strategy (recommend concession/narrowing — this is the cheapest fix in the case):**
1. Revise ¶ 18 to (a) limit the release to claims **arising out of or relating to the conduct of the sale process or the negotiation, execution, and performance of the APA and the Sale Order**, (b) expressly **carve out Proof of Claim No. 247 and any claim relating to the MFA or its termination**, and (c) expressly preserve all estate causes of action (which are Excluded Assets under APA § 2.2(b) in any event).
2. Confirm that no party is required to release claims as a condition to bid participation beyond what the Bid Procedures Order already approved.
3. Framing at the hearing: the Debtor never intended the release to reach pre-petition creditor claims like Tidewater's; the carve-out makes that explicit and moots the objection.

## IV. ADDITIONAL CONSIDERATIONS

1. **The termination merits (for the claim objection, not the sale):** MFA § 12.3 gives a **45-day** cure period from the September 22, 2023 Default Notice, and cure of a Payment Default requires payment **plus accrued interest** (1.5%/month under § 7.1). Tidewater wired $1.185M on October 20 (day 28) — facially timely — but apparently **without accrued interest**, which the Debtor can argue rendered the cure incomplete. Separately, MFA § 12.4 requires a Termination Notice with an effective date **10–30 days after the notice**, yet the November 15 termination was "effective immediately" — a potential procedural defect the Debtor must be prepared to address in claims litigation (and which Tidewater has not yet emphasized). These merits issues do not affect the sale (Excluded Asset/Excluded Liabilities structure) but frame the end-game: Tidewater's realistic recovery path is a GUC, and Waverly's ~$30.2M deficiency claim will crowd the unsecured pool — useful context for potential resolution discussions.
2. **Damages vulnerability in Proof of Claim No. 247:** Component 1 ("Lost Franchise Fee Savings," $2.97M) is conceptually incoherent — fees Tidewater *would have paid* Pinnacle are a cost, not a damage; claiming them as "savings" while also claiming the franchise was valuable is double-counting/contradictory. Component 2 relies on an "incremental brand profit" estimate of $851,111/yr with no disclosed methodology. Both are strong objection targets.
3. **Record discrepancies to reconcile before the hearing:** (a) marketing metrics (Sale Motion: 85 contacted / 42 NDAs / 18 in data room / 7 IOs vs. UCC: 120 / 23 / data room / 5 IOs); (b) docket-number inconsistencies (Sale Motion cites Bid Procedures Order as Dkt. 142; Objection and Bid Procedures Order context suggest Dkt. 156/148 — confirm the correct docket number); (c) committee counsel firm name appears inconsistently (Cromdale Consulting Whitfield LLP vs. Mercer Whitfield LLP). None is substantive, but tidy them.
4. **Standing/estoppel theme:** Tidewater received the Bid Procedures Order notice, objected to nothing in May, missed the bid deadline, and now — four days before the sale hearing — seeks to relitigate protections and inject itself as a bidder. A coherent narrative of procedural default across all six objections strengthens the Debtor's position.
