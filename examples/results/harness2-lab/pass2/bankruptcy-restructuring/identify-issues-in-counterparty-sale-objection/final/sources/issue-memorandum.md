# ISSUE MEMORANDUM — PRIVILEGED & CONFIDENTIAL / ATTORNEY WORK PRODUCT

**To:** Restructuring Team (Counsel to Pinnacle Hospitality Group, Inc.)
**Re:** *In re Pinnacle Hospitality Group, Inc.*, Case No. 24-41387-KLP (Bankr. E.D. Va.) — Analysis of Tidewater Lodging Ventures, LLC's Objection to the Sale Motion [Dkt. No. 214] and Response Strategy
**Sale Hearing:** July 29, 2024, 10:00 a.m. (ET), Hon. Katherine L. Prescott

## I. Purpose and Scope

This memorandum analyzes, argument by argument, the six grounds asserted in the Objection of Tidewater Lodging Ventures, LLC ("Tidewater") to the Debtor's Motion to approve the sale of substantially all assets to Grandview Capital Partners, LLC ("Grandview") under 11 U.S.C. §§ 363(b) and 363(f), and recommends a response strategy for the Sale Hearing. It draws on the Sale Motion, the APA Summary, the Bid Procedures Order, the MFA excerpts, Tidewater's Proof of Claim No. 247, and the UCC's support statement.

**Note on source documents:** All eight listed documents were reviewed except `insider-disclosure-email.eml`, which could not be converted/read after two attempts (conversion error). Because the insider issue (Objection Ground 3) is material, facts concerning the Haines–Townsend relationship are taken from the Objection itself (¶¶ 26–29), the Sale Motion (¶¶ 28–29, 47 as described), and the UCC statement (§ III.A). The email should be obtained and reviewed before the Sale Hearing.

## II. Summary of Recommendations

All six Tidewater arguments should be contested, and we expect to prevail on five; the release provision (Ground 6) is the one area where a clarifying carve-out is the prudent concession. In brief:

1. **§ 363(f) (Ground 1):** Tidewater's franchise interests are Excluded Assets under APA § 2.2(d); § 363(f) is not implicated as to Tidewater at all. Strongest response.
2. **Break-up fee (Ground 2):** Approved by final, unappealed Bid Procedures Order after notice and no objection; law-of-the-case/waiver.
3. **Insider status (Ground 3):** Relationship was disclosed; recusal, independent banker, and UCC oversight rebut non-arm's-length inference. Moderate risk; prepare evidentiary support for recusal.
4. **§ 365 cure rights (Ground 4):** Moot/misconceived — nothing is being assumed or assigned as to Tidewater; no cure rights arise.
5. **Late $160M bid (Ground 5):** Facially deficient: below the $164,255,000 minimum qualified bid, untimely (bid deadline July 15, 2024), and unsupported by deposit/financing evidence.
6. **Sale Order ¶ 18 release (Ground 6):** Narrow or clarify the release to make explicit it does not release estate claims against the Debtor or extinguish proofs of claim; this removes Tidewater's strongest facial point at minimal cost.

## III. Argument-by-Argument Analysis and Response Strategy

### Ground 1 — Sale Does Not Satisfy § 363(f) as to Tidewater's Franchise Interest (Objection ¶¶ 30–33)

**Tidewater's position:** None of the five § 363(f) conditions is satisfied as to its "interest" in the MFA; it does not consent; franchise rights cannot be forced to money satisfaction.

**Assessment: Weak. The structural answer is dispositive.** The APA expressly excludes the Terminated Franchise Agreements from the Purchased Assets (APA § 2.2(d); APA Summary § IV(d)). As the APA Summary states, because the Terminated Franchise Agreements are Excluded Assets, "the sale does not purport to transfer, convey, or extinguish any franchise interest of Tidewater," and "the mechanism of Section 363(f) ... is not implicated with respect to Tidewater's alleged franchise relationship because that relationship ... is simply not part of the transaction." Section 363(f) strips interests only from property *being sold*; Tidewater's asserted interest (whether a surviving contract or a damages claim) remains with the estate and is untouched.

**Fallback argument:** Even if § 363(f) were reached, Tidewater's alleged interest is, at minimum, in **bona fide dispute** under § 363(f)(4): the Debtor terminated the MFA effective November 15, 2023 after expiration of the § 12.3 cure period (Sale Motion ¶ 20), and the Debtor intends to object to Proof of Claim No. 247 (Sale Motion ¶ 21). Note that Tidewater itself concedes in ¶ 33(d) that "the validity of the MFA termination may be in dispute," which substantially undermines its insistence that § 363(f)(4) cannot apply. Also note a latent timing tension in Tidewater's papers: the MFA provides a **45-day cure period from the Default Notice** (MFA § 12.3); Tidewater's October 20, 2023 wire was 28 days after the September 22, 2023 Default Notice — facially within the period — but the Default Notice was preceded by Pinnacle's August 2023 assertion of the deficiency, and the MFA's Payment Default trigger runs from when payment is *due* (15 days; § 12.1(a)), not from notice. Consider developing the record on when the Q2/Q3 amounts actually became due and whether the Debtor's termination theory rests on the notice date or the original payment due dates. Regardless, the merits of the termination are for claims administration, not the sale.

**Response strategy:** Lead with the Excluded-Asset structure; argue in the alternative under § 363(f)(4). Emphasize that Tidewater's remedy is Proof of Claim No. 247, which is fully preserved (see Ground 6 carve-out).

### Ground 2 — Break-Up Fee Excessive and Chilled Bidding (Objection ¶ 5, "Second")

**Tidewater's position:** The $4.755M break-up fee (3.0% of the $158.5M purchase price) is excessive and chilled bidding, evidenced by the absence of qualified competing bids.

**Assessment: Weak; largely waived.** The Break-Up Fee and $1.5M Expense Reimbursement were expressly approved by the Bid Procedures Order entered May 20, 2024, after notice to all parties in interest and a May 17, 2024 hearing at which **no party objected** (Bid Procedures Order ¶ F). The Court made express findings that the fee is reasonable, consistent with market practice, and "will not unduly chill competitive bidding" (¶¶ 12, 14; aggregate protections of $6.255M ≈ 3.95% of the purchase price found within range). Tidewater filed no objection to the Bid Procedures Motion. Arguments that the approved protections were excessive could and should have been raised then; they are waived and are law of the case.

**On the merits:** The marketing process was robust — Langford Rae contacted 85+ potential bidders (Sale Motion ¶ 24; the UCC cites 120+), 42 signed NDAs, 18 accessed the data room, and seven preliminary indications of interest were received (Sale Motion ¶ 26). Parties declining to bid cited capex needs, market conditions, and DIP timing — not the fee (Sale Motion ¶ 32). A 3.0% break-up fee is within customary market range, as the Court already found.

**Response strategy:** Waiver/law of the case first; merits second, using the outreach statistics and the reasons non-bidders gave. Enlist the UCC's independent finding that the process was "fair, open, transparent, and conducted at arm's length" (UCC Statement §§ I, III.A).

### Ground 3 — Grandview Is an Insider; Heightened Scrutiny (Objection ¶¶ 26–29)

**Tidewater's position:** Gerald Haines, Grandview's Managing Director leading the acquisition, is married to Catherine Haines (née Townsend), sister of Debtor CEO Marcus Townsend; disclosure and recusal are inadequate; the process was structured to favor Grandview.

**Assessment: The most rhetorically powerful objection, but rebuttable.** Key responsive facts from the record: (a) the relationship was **voluntarily disclosed** in ¶ 47 of the Sale Motion and ¶ 23 of the Bid Procedures Motion (conceded by Tidewater, Objection ¶ 28); (b) Townsend **recused from all Grandview negotiations and from the board vote** approving the APA (Sale Motion ¶¶ 28–29; UCC Statement § III.A); (c) COO Patricia Medina led the process, assisted by independent banker Langford Rae and Debtor's counsel; (d) the **UCC independently investigated** the process — its counsel had data room access, received all IOIs and bid letters, attended negotiation sessions, and was consulted pre-execution (Sale Motion ¶¶ 31; UCC Statement §§ I, III.A) — and concluded the safeguards were adequate; and (e) Grandview's bid was the **highest among seven preliminary indications of interest** (Sale Motion ¶¶ 26, 30(a)).

Tidewater's counters — that recusal is "self-reported," Medina reports to Townsend, and Langford Rae was retained pre-recusal — are speculative and do not establish non-arm's-length dealing. Grandview is also not a statutory "insider" of the Debtor under § 101(31) on these facts (no identified governance, control, or employment relationship); a brother-in-law of the CEO, standing alone, does not make the *buyer* an insider of the *debtor*.

**Risk factor:** The Debtor bears the burden of showing arm's-length process and fair value where connections exist. The unread `insider-disclosure-email.eml` may contain relevant facts (e.g., the disclosure timeline or recusal mechanics) — obtain and review it before the hearing.

**Response strategy:** Support the Sale Motion with declarations from Medina and a Langford Rae representative attesting to (i) Townsend's complete non-involvement, (ii) the competitive selection of Grandview among seven IOIs, and (iii) the banker's independent role. Highlight UCC's independent investigation and endorsement. Offer, if the Court is concerned, to include an express factual finding of arm's-length negotiation in the Sale Order.

### Ground 4 — APA Exclusion of Terminated Franchise Agreements Deprives Tidewater of § 365 Cure Rights (Objection ¶ 5, "Fourth")

**Tidewater's position:** Excluding the Terminated Franchise Agreements from the Purchased Assets improperly deprives Tidewater of cure rights under § 365.

**Assessment: Internally inconsistent and weak.** This argument contradicts Ground 1: Tidewater simultaneously argues (a) the sale *strips* its franchise interests under § 363(f) and (b) the MFA should be *included* in the sale so it can assert § 365 rights. Section 365 cure rights belong to the **debtor** (and, for assumption, run with contract counterparties only if the contract is assumed and assigned). Nothing is being assumed or assigned as to Tidewater: the MFA is not on the Assumed Contracts Schedule, and the APA's Excluded Liabilities expressly retain "any and all liabilities arising from or related to the Terminated Franchise Agreements, the MFA, or the Tidewater dispute" with the estate (APA Summary § V). Tidewater has no statutory entitlement to force the estate either to sell or to assume its contract; rejection/claims-process treatment is the debtor's business judgment.

**Response strategy:** Point out the internal contradiction with Ground 1; explain that Tidewater's asserted rights are fully preserved as an unsecured claim against the estate, which retains (and has not transferred) all Tidewater-related liabilities. The exclusion *protects* Tidewater, not the reverse.

### Ground 5 — Tidewater's Late $160.0 Million Competing Bid (Objection ¶ 5, "Fifth")

**Tidewater's position:** It should be permitted to submit a late bid of $160.0M, exceeding the $158.5M stalking horse bid, to maximize value.

**Assessment: Deficient on multiple independent grounds.**
1. **Below the minimum qualified bid.** The Bid Procedures Order sets a floor of **$164,255,000** ($158.5M + $4.755M break-up fee + $1M initial overbid) (¶ 5(a)). The $160M proposal is more than $4.2M short — as the UCC's statement expressly notes (UCC Statement § V).
2. **Untimely.** The Bid Deadline was July 15, 2024, 5:00 p.m. ET; the Order states late bids "shall not be considered ... for any purpose ... unless the Court, upon motion and for cause shown, orders otherwise" (¶ 6). Tidewater made no showing of cause — it was aware of the process (it filed its Proof of Claim May 1, 2024 and was served with notice) and offers no explanation for its delay. Its objection was itself filed on the July 22 objection deadline.
3. **No compliance showing.** A qualified bid requires a $15.85M good-faith deposit, proof of committed financing, an executed mark-up of the APA, and no contingencies (¶ 5(b)–(f)). Tidewater's objection asserts a number only, with no deposit, financing evidence, or APA mark-up.
4. **Net value is illusory.** A $160M bid triggering payment of the $4.755M break-up fee (plus up to $1.5M expense reimbursement) to Grandview yields no improvement for the estate and would jeopardize the September 30, 2024 DIP Maturity Milestone — default under which could trigger acceleration, loss of cash collateral use, and potential Chapter 7 conversion (Sale Motion ¶ 17; Bid Procedures Order ¶ C; UCC Statement § II.B).

**Response strategy:** Oppose firmly on all four grounds; emphasize prejudice to the estate and to the 2,800 employees of any timeline slippage. Tidewater's real objective appears to be leverage on its $6.8M disputed claim, not a genuine purchase.

### Ground 6 — Sale Order ¶ 18 Non-Consensual Release (Objection ¶¶ 5 "Sixth," 23)

**Tidewater's position:** Paragraph 18 of the proposed Sale Order releases "the Buyer, Seller, and their respective officers, directors, agents, and employees ... from any and all claims ... arising out of or related to the Sale Transaction," and could be read to extinguish Tidewater's $6.8M Proof of Claim No. 247 against the estate.

**Assessment: Tidewater's most credible textual point, easily fixed.** Read literally, a release running to the "Seller" (the Debtor) "and its officers, directors, agents, and employees" from "any and all claims ... related to the Sale Transaction" is broader than necessary and, depending on reading, could arguably sweep in estate-asserted claims or impair claims-process rights. Fifth Circuit-law (this being an EDVA case under In re MPH and related authority) disfavors non-consensual third-party releases absent specific findings, and courts routinely narrow "free and clear" sale orders so they do not adjudicate claims allowance. Note also that Tidewater's wrongful-termination claim does not "arise out of or relate to the Sale Transaction" (it predates the petition), so the release should not reach it in any event — but ambiguity invites litigation.

**Response strategy:** Concede a clarifying amendment: revise ¶ 18 (or add a carve-out) stating that the release (a) runs only to claims *arising out of or relating to the Sale Transaction itself*, (b) does not release the Debtor or the estate from any claim asserted or assertable in the claims-allowance process, including Proof of Claim No. 247, and (c) is binding only on parties that consent or whose claims relate to the transferred assets. This moots Ground 6 at zero cost to the estate and undermines Tidewater's standing narrative. The Debtor retains full rights to object to Claim No. 247 on the merits (Sale Motion ¶ 21).

## IV. Cross-Cutting Themes for the Hearing

1. **Preservation of Tidewater's remedies.** The transactional structure (Excluded Assets + Excluded Liabilities + ¶ 18 carve-out) leaves Tidewater exactly where bankruptcy law puts it: a general unsecured creditor with a disputed $6.8M claim to be adjudicated in claims administration. Its objection seeks a remedy (sale denial or modification) unrelated to any actual prejudice.
2. **Estate-wide harm from delay.** The DIP Maturity Milestone (September 30, 2024), the $22M DIP facility from Waverly (the prepetition lender owed ~$171.2M, leaving a ~$30.2M deficiency claim against the estate per the APA Summary), and the risk of Chapter 7 conversion make timeline certainty a creditor-body interest, not merely a lender interest (UCC Statement §§ II.B, IV).
3. **UCC alignment.** The UCC's independent investigation, support statement, and point-by-point rejection of Tidewater's arguments (including the bid-deficiency math) should be featured prominently; the UCC — not Tidewater — is the fiduciary for unsecured creditors collectively.
4. **Evidentiary preparation.** Secure declarations from Patricia Medina and Langford Rae on the arm's-length process and Grandview selection; obtain and review the insider-disclosure email; assemble the bidder-outreach statistics and non-bidder exit reasons.
5. **Merits of the termination dispute (for later).** Build the claim-objection record now: MFA § 12.3's 45-day cure period, the payment due dates under § 7.1 (monthly, 15th of following month) versus the notice date, and interest accrual under § 7.1 (cure of a Payment Default requires payment in full "together with any accrued interest"). Also probe the damages model in Proof of Claim No. 247 — Component 1 ("lost franchise fee *savings*") claims as damages the fees Tidewater would have *paid* Pinnacle, an offsetting-cost theory of dubious validity, and Component 2's $851,111/yr incremental-brand-profit estimate is unsupported by any produced analysis.
