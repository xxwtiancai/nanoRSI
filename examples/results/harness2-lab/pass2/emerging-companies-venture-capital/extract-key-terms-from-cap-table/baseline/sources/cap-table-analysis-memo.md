## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Cascade Robotics, Inc. (Delaware C-corp; EIN 83-4127650) proposes a $45,000,000 Series C financing at a $180,000,000 pre-money / $225,000,000 post-money valuation, with Ridgeline Growth Partners Fund VI, L.P. investing $35,000,000 and existing preferred holders (Firstvale Ventures and Summit Arc Capital) taking $10,000,000 of pro rata participation. The term sheet fixes the pre-money fully diluted capitalization at 22,004,000 shares, yielding a price of $8.18 per share and approximately 5,500,000 Series C shares.

**Material findings requiring attention before the November 22, 2024 board meeting:**

1. **The 22,004,000-share fully diluted denominator appears understated.** It omits (a) 450,000 shares of common stock issued upon option exercises, (b) 320,616 shares of Series A Preferred issued on conversion of the Firstvale convertible note (per the Restated COI, 3,820,616 Series A shares are outstanding, not 3,500,000), and (c) 150,000 warrant shares. A corrected denominator is approximately **22,924,616 shares**, implying a price of ~**$7.85** rather than $8.18 — a difference of roughly 230,000+ Series C shares to investors.
2. **Internal inconsistency in the term sheet on the option pool increase.** Section 1.10 requires a 2,500,000-share pool increase (5,000,000 → 7,500,000) to be *included* in pre-money fully diluted capitalization, but the 22,004,000-share figure in Section 1.6 uses only the legacy pool. If Section 1.10 is honored, the denominator rises by a further 2,500,000 shares and the price falls to ~$7.34 (on the term sheet's own numbers) or ~$7.08 (on corrected numbers).
3. **Available pool discrepancy.** The cap table summary shows 1,275,000 available pool shares (5,000,000 − 3,725,000 granted); the fully diluted buildup uses 1,204,000; the 2019 EIP's forfeiture-reversion provision (Section 2.3) suggests the true available reserve may be higher still. Must be reconciled before closing; the Company's cap table accuracy is a closing condition (Term Sheet §§ 1.11, 5.6).
4. **Series C terms are materially investor-favorable**: 1.5x non-participating liquidation preference, **full ratchet** anti-dilution (vs. broad-based weighted average on Series A/B), and unusually sweeping protective provisions (Series C consent required for *any* equity plan issuance — even previously board-approved grants — budget approval, and C-suite hiring/compensation changes).
5. **409A anomaly.** The 9/30/2024 409A valuation sets common stock FMV at $8.75 — *above* the $8.18 Series C preferred price. Because the Series C term sheet was not signed as of the 409A valuation date, the report took no account of it; a new 409A valuation should be obtained promptly after signing/closing.

### 3.4 Liquidation waterfall (post-Series C)

1. **Series C first**: 1.5x non-participating preference = $12.27/share; aggregate ≈ **$67.5M** (5,500,000 × $12.27) ahead of all other classes.
2. **Series A and B, pari passu**: Series A $10.5M (3,500,000 × $3.00; ~$11.5M if 3,820,616 shares) + Series B $25.2M (4,200,000 × $6.00) = **~$35.7–36.7M** aggregate.
3. **Common** receives the residual. Series A/B/C holders may convert to common in lieu of taking their preference.

- Total stacked preference post-close: **~$103M** (vs. ~$35.7M currently).
- Automatic conversion of Series C requires a Qualified IPO (≥$50M gross proceeds at ≥$16.36/share, i.e., 2x OIP); Series A mandatory conversion at ≥$30M proceeds and ≥$9.00/share (3x OIP).

### 3.5 409A interplay

The 9/30/2024 409A valuation (OPM; total equity value ~$145M; 65% volatility; 4.52% risk-free rate; 3.0-year horizon; 25% DLOM) concluded common FMV of **$8.75/share**. This exceeds the proposed $8.18 Series C preferred price — economically anomalous, since preferred carrying a 1.5x preference should be worth at least as much per share as common. The 409A predates the term sheet (the report expressly notes no term sheet had been signed as of the valuation date), so the Series C will be a material subsequent event. **Recommendation:** obtain a fresh 409A valuation promptly; note also that the 409A's $145M equity value sits well below the $180M pre-money, a gap the board should understand when approving the round and any new option grants.

## 4. DISCREPANCIES AND OPEN ISSUES

**4.1 Series A share count (high priority).** COI §§ 4.2(b) and 5A.1 state 3,820,616 Series A shares outstanding (including 320,616 note-conversion shares); the cap table, term sheet § 1.6(ii), and the Valuation History tab show only 3,500,000. Either the COI is wrong or the cap table and term sheet denominator are wrong. This directly affects the price-per-share calculation and the Company's cap table accuracy representation.

**4.2 Available option pool (high priority).** Three inconsistent figures: 1,275,000 (Summary tab: 5,000,000 − 3,725,000 granted), 1,204,000 (FD buildup and term sheet § 1.6(v)), and a potentially higher figure under EIP § 2.3 (forfeited and exercised shares revert to the reserve). Since the term sheet uses the pool count in the price denominator, this must be reconciled exactly.

**4.3 Term sheet internal inconsistency on pool increase (high priority).** § 1.6 vs. § 1.10, as quantified in § 3.2 above.

**4.4 Warrant description conflicts.** Cap table and 409A report: Pinnacle National Bank warrant dated 6/22/2021, $3.00 exercise. COI § 4.2(e): warrant dated 3/10/2022 at $3.50. Confirm the correct instrument from the warrant register and the underlying Loan and Security Agreement.

**4.5 COI recital of options outstanding.** COI § 4.2(d) recites ~2,800,000 options outstanding as of 8/12/2022 vs. 3,100,000 per the current grant schedule — plausible given post-2022 grants, but the Series A count in § 4.2(b) cannot be explained by timing and is a genuine conflict.

**4.6 Founder name.** "Raj Venkatesh" (term sheet, cap table, 409A) vs. "Raj Subramanian" (CNPA recitals; Voting Agreement definition of Key Holders). Verify legal name and conform the stock ledger, voting agreement exhibits, and Series C closing documents.

**4.7 Board designee names.** Cap table Series A/B tabs list board designees as Thomas Aldridge and Victoria Langford; the COI, IRA, and Voting Agreement uniformly identify Allison Trang (Firstvale) and Derek Hollowell (Summit Arc). Correct the cap table. Note the Series C financing contemplates a Ridgeline director and Series C consent rights over board size (Term Sheet § 2.5(j)), requiring amendment of the five-member board structure in Voting Agreement § 2.1 — protected provisions requiring consent of each designating party and the affected preferred series (VA § 6.1).

**4.8 Series C protective provisions vs. EIP.** Term Sheet § 2.5(b) requires Series C majority consent for *any* equity issuance "including any shares issued pursuant to the Company's equity incentive plan or any option... granted under any equity incentive plan, whether or not previously approved by the Board." This directly conflicts with EIP §§ 3.2 and 6.1 (Administrator discretion within the approved reserve; no per-series consent for routine grants) and effectively gives Ridgeline a veto over all hiring-related equity. Also beyond market: Series C consent rights over the annual budget and expenditures >10% of budget, debt >$5M, dividends, repurchases, and hiring/termination/compensation of any C-level executive (§§ 2.5(f)–(k)) — well beyond Series A/B protections.

**4.9 Full ratchet anti-dilution.** Series C carries full ratchet (with enumerated exclusions; note the equity-plan and M&A carve-outs require the affirmative vote of a Series C-designated director); Series A/B are broad-based weighted average. Full ratchet materially increases downside dilution risk to common holders in any down round.

**4.10 Pro rata participation mechanics.** The $10M existing-investor allocation is governed by the IRA pro rata provisions; unsubscribed amounts may be reallocated to Ridgeline "at the Lead Investor's discretion." Firstvale and Summit Arc each qualify as a Major Investor (≥1,000,000 preferred shares) under IRA § 1.14.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Reconcile the fully diluted denominator before signing definitive documents**, incorporating (i) 450,000 exercised-option shares, (ii) 320,616 Series A note-conversion shares, (iii) 150,000 warrant shares, and (iv) the 2,500,000-share pool increase, and restate the Original Issue Price accordingly (indicatively ~$7.08–$7.85 depending on treatment of the pool).
2. **Resolve the available-pool figure** (1,204,000 vs. 1,275,000 vs. EIP § 2.3 reversion math) with transfer agent Ashford Financial Services and counsel.
3. **Negotiate the § 2.5 protective provisions**, particularly deletion or narrowing of the equity-plan consent right (§ 2.5(b)) and the operational consents (budget, executive hiring), and push for broad-based weighted average instead of full-ratchet anti-dilution.
4. **Order a new 409A valuation** after term sheet signing; a common FMV ($8.75) above the preferred price ($8.18) is unsustainable post-signing and affects option pricing.
5. **Clean up record discrepancies**: founder legal name (Venkatesh/Subramanian), warrant date/price, board designee names on the cap table, and the Series A share count in all closing documents and the new Restated Charter.
6. **Confirm pro rata allocations** under the IRA between Firstvale and Summit Arc and document reallocation mechanics for any unsubscribed portion.
7. **Model the ~$103M stacked liquidation preference** and full-ratchet down-round scenarios against realistic exit values when presenting the deal to the board and common holders.
