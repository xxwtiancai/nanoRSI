## 3. Key Drafting Decisions

- **Immediate irrevocability.** Clients relay that Keisha wants the consent "final and irrevocable immediately." The draft records her wish (§ 5) but does not purport to waive any statutory revocation window, as such waivers are generally ineffective under Maryland law. The revocation period should be confirmed against the current statute/court practice and explained to Keisha in writing.
- **Attorney presence at execution.** Keisha is represented by Danica Okafor, Esq. The notarial certificate includes a checkbox certifying whether counsel was present, consistent with Maryland independent-adoption consent practice; confirm whether Ms. Okafor will attend the signing at our office (target on/about June 10, 2024).
- **Post-adoption contact.** The informal visitation understanding (holidays/birthdays) is addressed in § 6 of the consent as **not a condition of, and not enforceable through, the consent**. If the parties want an enforceable arrangement, a separate post-adoption contact agreement must be considered. This remains an open client question (intake memo, Open Items ¶ 7) and the unread `client-visitation-email.eml` is directly relevant.
- **Child support / arrearages.** Father's counsel (Gerald Tate, Esq.) reportedly stated Darnell's consent "resolves all obligations related to the child." The draft expressly disclaims any effect of the consent on the existing support order (Case No. 19-FS-0008714; ~$14,421 in arrearages as of March 1, 2024) or on TANF-related obligations assigned to the State (TANF received Oct. 2021–June 2023).
- **Father's consent.** Darnell Whitfield executed a notarized Consent and Relinquishment on March 1, 2024, at Jessup Correctional Institution, witnessed by prison officials. Obtain and review the original before relying on it (intake Open Items ¶ 2).
