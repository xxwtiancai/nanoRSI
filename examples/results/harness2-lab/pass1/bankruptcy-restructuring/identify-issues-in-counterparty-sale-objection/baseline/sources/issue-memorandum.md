# ISSUE MEMORANDUM — ANALYSIS OF TIDEWATER OBJECTION TO 363 SALE

**In re Pinnacle Hospitality Group, Inc.**, Case No. 24-41387-KLP (Bankr. E.D. Va., Richmond Div.) (Prescott, J.)

**Re:** Tidewater Lodging Ventures, LLC Objection to Sale Motion (Dkt. No. 214); Sale Hearing July 29, 2024

**Purpose:** Argument-by-argument analysis of the Tidewater Objection, with recommended response strategy for the Debtor.

---

## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Tidewater raises six objections: (1) sale cannot proceed free and clear under § 363(f); (2) the break-up fee is excessive and chilled bidding; (3) Grandview is an insider requiring heightened scrutiny; (4) exclusion of the Terminated Franchise Agreements deprives Tidewater of § 365 cure rights; (5) Tidewater should be permitted to submit a late $160 million bid; and (6) Paragraph 18 of the proposed Sale Order contains an impermissible non-consensual release.

**Bottom line:** Five of the six objections (Nos. 2–6) are substantively weak and answerable on the existing record — the bid is facially deficient under the Bid Procedures Order, the insider issue was disclosed with recusal and UCC sign-off, the APA's exclusion of the MFA actually *preserves* Tidewater's claims, and any release can be narrowed. **Objection No. 1 (§ 363(f)) is the most dangerous** because it is tied to the merits of the wrongful-termination dispute, and the documents suggest Tidewater's cure argument has real force (payment 28 days into a 45-day cure period under MFA § 12.3). The recommended strategy is to moot Objection No. 1 structurally — i.e., confirm that the sale does not seek to strip any Tidewater "interest" because the MFA is an Excluded Asset, and that Tidewater's claims ride through to the claims-allowance process — rather than to litigate the validity of the termination at the Sale Hearing.

---

## II. BACKGROUND FACTS (KEY TO ALL ARGUMENTS)

- **MFA:** executed June 1, 2018; 10-year term to May 31, 2028. Fees: 5.5% royalty, 2.0% marketing fund, $150,000/hotel/year technology fee (~$2.97M/year at $31.6M FY2023 gross room revenue). **Cure period: 45 days from Default Notice (MFA § 12.3), applicable to all defaults including Payment Defaults.**
- **Default/termination chronology:** Default Notice September 22, 2023 (~$1.185M for Q2/Q3 2023 royalties + marketing); Tidewater wired $1.185M on **October 20, 2023 — day 28 of a 45-day cure period**; Pinnacle terminated the MFA effective November 15, 2023, asserting the cure was untimely. The Termination Letter's timeliness theory is facially inconsistent with § 12.3. (Note: § 12.3 also requires accrued interest under § 7.1 with any Payment Default cure — the Debtor's fallback position is that the $1.185M wire excluded accrued interest, so cure was incomplete; this is a fact-intensive dispute.)
- **Sale:** APA dated June 1, 2024 with Grandview; $158.5M total ($141M cash + $17.5M assumed liabilities: $11.2M cure costs + $6.3M trade payables). Break-up fee $4.755M (3.0%) plus expense reimbursement capped at $1.5M (aggregate $6.255M, ~3.95%). Approved by Bid Procedures Order entered May 20, 2024, **after notice and a May 17, 2024 hearing at which no party objected**, including Tidewater.
- **Bid procedures:** Bid Deadline July 15, 2024, 5:00 p.m. ET; **minimum Qualified Bid $164,255,000** ($158.5M + $4.755M break-up fee + $1M initial overbid); 10% deposit ($15.85M); no contingencies; proof of committed financing. No qualified competing bids received.
- **MFA is an Excluded Asset** (APA § 2.2(d)); Assumed Contracts Schedule does not include the MFA; all Tidewater-related liabilities are Excluded Liabilities. Buyer takes no rights under the MFA.
- **Insider relationship:** Gerald Haines (Grandview MD, deal lead) is married to Catherine Haines née Townsend, sister of Debtor CEO Marcus Townsend. Disclosed in Sale Motion ¶47 and Bid Procedures Motion ¶23. Townsend recused; COO Patricia Medina led negotiations; board approved APA unanimously May 30, 2024 (Townsend recused).
- **Proof of Claim No. 247:** $6.8M GUC — $2.97M "lost franchise fee savings" (one year) + $3.83M lost profits ($851,111/yr × 4.5 years). Filed May 1, 2024.
- **UCC:** Appointed April 2, 2024; investigated the process; filed a supporting statement July 25, 2024 expressly rejecting each Tidewater argument.

## III. ARGUMENT-BY-ARGUMENT ANALYSIS

### Objection 1 — § 363(f): Sale Cannot Strip Tidewater's Franchise Interest Without Consent

**Tidewater's position:** It has not consented; none of the five § 363(f) conditions is satisfied as to its franchise "interest."

**Analysis:** This is the objection with the greatest legal surface area, but it is largely **misdirected** on this record:

1. **The MFA is not being sold.** APA § 2.2(d) expressly excludes the Terminated Franchise Agreements from the Purchased Assets, and the Assumed Contracts Schedule omits them. Section 363(f) strips interests only *from property being sold*. Because the franchise relationship (whatever its status) is not part of the transaction, § 363(f) is not being invoked against Tidewater's franchise interest at all. The APA Summary states this expressly: "the proposed sale free and clear under Section 363(f) ... is not implicated with respect to Tidewater's alleged franchise relationship."
2. **Tidewater's real remedy rides through.** If the termination was wrongful, Tidewater holds a $6.8M claim against the estate (as it asserts in Claim No. 247) — which is unaffected by the sale — or, in the alternative, retains whatever in personam contract rights it holds against the estate, which remains the counterparty because Grandview assumes nothing under the MFA. Either way, nothing of Tidewater's is extinguished.
3. **Residual risk — trademarks.** The Purchased Assets include the Pinnacle Inn marks and reservation/technology systems. If Tidewater prevails on wrongful termination, it could claim a continuing right to *use* the marks and systems — rights that would be burdened by a free-and-clear sale of the IP. Two responses: (a) under § 363(f)(4), the estate's position is that any such claimed right is, at minimum, in **bona fide dispute** (the Debtor contends the termination was valid; Tidewater contends it was not — that is the paradigm of a bona fide dispute); and (b) even Tidewater's objection concedes a dispute exists as to the *termination's validity* while disputing the *existence* of an interest — but a disputed franchisee-use right in the marks is squarely a disputed "interest," satisfying (f)(4).
4. **Caution:** Do not litigate the merits of the termination at the Sale Hearing. The documentary record (28-day cure within a 45-day window) favors Tidewater on timeliness; the Debtor's stronger ground is the accrued-interest incompleteness argument, which is a claims-process issue, not a sale-approval issue.

**Response strategy:** Argue that § 363(f) is not implicated because the MFA is an Excluded Asset; alternatively, § 363(f)(4) (bona fide dispute) independently authorizes any free-and-clear effect as to the IP. Offer Sale Order language expressly preserving Claim No. 247 and any Tidewater contract/claims against the *estate* (not the Buyer), which moots the objection without conceding the termination dispute. Classify this objection **high risk if mishandled / low risk if framed structurally.**

### Objection 2 — Break-Up Fee Excessive; Chilled Bidding

**Tidewater's position:** $4.755M (3.0%) break-up fee, and $6.255M total protections, are excessive and chilled bidding, evidenced by zero qualified bids.

**Analysis:** Weak.

- The break-up fee and expense reimbursement were **approved by this Court on May 20, 2024 after notice and a hearing**, with express findings of reasonableness and consistency with market practice (Bid Procedures Order ¶¶ 12–14). Tidewater did not object then; arguments could and should have been raised at the bid-procedures stage (law-of-the-case / waiver / laches).
- The 3.0% break-up fee is within customary market ranges (courts commonly approve 3–4% plus capped expenses); the aggregate ~3.95% was expressly found reasonable.
- Absence of bids is equally explained by the Debtor's documented market conditions: capital-expenditure requirements, competitive market softness, and DIP timeline — as communicated by parties that declined to bid (Sale Motion ¶ 32).
- **Evidence discrepancy to manage:** the Sale Motion describes 85+ parties contacted / 42 NDAs / 18 in the data room, while the UCC statement describes 120 contacted / 23 NDAs / 5 IOIs. This inconsistency should be reconciled (e.g., declaration from Langford Rae) before the hearing so it cannot be used to attack process credibility.

**Response strategy:** Lead with waiver/finality of the Bid Procedures Order; cite the Court's own reasonableness findings; support with a Langford Rae declaration on marketing breadth (reconciling the outreach numbers) and on why non-bidders declined. **Low risk.**

### Objection 3 — Grandview Is an Insider; Heightened Scrutiny

**Tidewater's position:** Haines (Grandview) is CEO Townsend's brother-in-law; disclosure and self-reported recusal are insufficient; Medina reports to Townsend; Langford Rae was retained under Townsend.

**Analysis:** Moderately weak but the most rhetorically potent.

- Grandview is not a statutory insider of the * Debtor* (no identity of interest, no non-arm's-length dealings creating insider status under § 101); the relationship is one degree removed (spouse of the CEO's sister). Even if heightened *scrutiny* applies, the standard factors favor the Debtor:
  - Full and timely disclosure (Sale Motion ¶47; Bid Procedures Motion ¶23) — no concealment.
  - Recusal of Townsend from negotiations, deliberations, and the board vote (board approved unanimously May 30, 2024, minus Townsend).
  - Medina, an officer since 2016 and COO since 2019, led negotiations with independent professionals.
  - An independent investment banker ran the process, and — critically — the **UCC conducted its own investigation** (data room logs, direct consultation with Langford Rae, attendance at negotiation sessions) and affirmatively concluded the process was arm's length.
  - Market check: multiple IOIs received; Grandview's price was the highest.
- Tidewater's rebuttal points (Medina's reporting line; banker retention timing) go to weight, not sufficiency, and are neutralized by the UCC's independent oversight and the court-approved auction framework that remained open to all comers.
- **Recommended belt-anduspenders:** offer evidence at the hearing corroborating the recusal (board minutes, Medina declaration, Langford Rae engagement chronology) so the recusal is not solely "self-reported."

**Response strategy:** Concede disclosure; emphasize independent oversight layers (banker + UCC + court-approved process + open auction); supply Medina and Langford Rae declarations. If the Court is concerned, propose findings reciting the safeguards rather than substantive changes. **Low-to-moderate risk.**

### Objection 4 — APA Exclusion of Terminated Franchise Agreements Deprives Tidewater of § 365 Cure Rights

**Tidewater's position:** Excluding the MFA from the Purchased Assets "circumvents" its rights as a counterparty to an executory contract, including cure rights under § 365.

**Analysis:** The weakest objection; it inverts the record.

- The Debtor terminated the MFA **pre-petition** (November 15, 2023). Whether the MFA is still an executory contract at all is the core disputed issue; if validly terminated, there is no contract to assume, reject, or cure.
- § 365 gives *the trustee* — not the counterparty — the right to assume or reject. Tidewater has no right to force assumption of its franchise agreement by the estate or assignment to the buyer.
- The exclusion **protects** Tidewater: Grandview acquires no MFA rights and assumes no related liabilities; Tidewater's Claim No. 247 and any contract claims survive against the estate and will be adjudicated in the claims process. If Tidewater wins the termination dispute, its remedy lies against the estate (damages or specific performance against the estate as retained obligor), unaffected by the sale.
- There is no "deprivation of cure rights": if the MFA were assumed and assigned, Tidewater would face the very outcome it says it fears (loss of its relationship to Pinnacle); if not, its damages claim stands.

**Response strategy:** Frame the exclusion as preserving, not eliminating, Tidewater's rights; note § 365 election rights belong to the debtor; point out Tidewater cannot simultaneously claim wrongful-termination damages *and* demand the contract remain in the estate for its benefit. **Low risk.**

### Objection 5 — Tidewater's Late $160 Million Competing Bid

**Tidewater's position:** It should be allowed to bid $160M late, exceeding the $158.5M stalking horse price and maximizing value.

**Analysis:** Facially deficient on three independent grounds:

1. **Below the minimum Qualified Bid.** $160.0M < $164,255,000 minimum (Bid Procedures Order ¶ 5(a)) — deficient by over $4.2M because it does not cover the break-up fee and initial overbid increment. The UCC statement makes the same point.
2. **Untimely.** The Bid Deadline was July 15, 2024, 5:00 p.m. ET, with time of the essence; late bids may be considered only on motion for cause. No cause is shown — Tidewater was a served party throughout, attended to the process sufficiently to object, and offers no excuse.
3. **No compliance showing.** No 10% deposit ($15.85M), no marked-up APA, no committed-financing evidence, no irrevocability — none of the Qualified Bid requirements of ¶ 5.
- The "maximize value" argument fails because a non-conforming, unfinanced, late expression of interest does not maximize value; it jeopardizes the closing timeline and the DIP Maturity Milestone (September 30, 2024), risking DIP default and potential Chapter 7 conversion — a catastrophic outcome for all creditors, as the UCC explains.

**Response strategy:** Oppose on each ground; rely on the Court's own Order and the *In re Dery* line that courts may strictly enforce bid procedures where the process was fair and noticed; highlight DIP milestone urgency. If the Court shows any inclination, the fallback is: any consideration of a late bid must require full deposit, financing proof, minimum $164.255M, and an adjournment stipulation protecting the break-up fee and timeline. **Low risk.**

### Objection 6 — Paragraph 18 Non-Consensual Release

**Tidewater's position:** The proposed Sale Order ¶ 18 releases the Buyer, Seller, and their officers/directors/agents/employees from "any and all claims ... arising out of or related to the Sale Transaction," which could extinguish Claim No. 247.

**Analysis:** Legitimate concern as drafted, easily fixed.

- Non-consensual third-party releases are impermissible in most circuits (and the Supreme Court's decision in *Purdue Pharma* (2024) confirms limits on non-consensual third-party releases in plans; sale orders are a fortiori narrower vehicles). A sale order may appropriately release * Buyer-related* claims (e.g., good-faith § 363(m) findings, exculpation for acts in the sale process), but not general creditor claims against the *estate*.
- Claim No. 247 arises from the MFA termination (November 2023), not the "Sale Transaction" — so even the drafted release arguably does not reach it — but the drafting ambiguity invites the fight.

**Response strategy:** Concede a narrowing modification: limit ¶ 18 to claims *arising out of or relating to the marketing, negotiation, and consummation of the Sale Transaction*, expressly excluding and preserving all pre-petition claims against the estate, including Claim No. 247. This costs the estate nothing (those claims are Excluded Liabilities/estate claims anyway) and removes Tidewater's cleanest procedural win. **Low risk if modified; moderate risk if the Debtor defends the current drafting.**

## IV. INTERNAL CONSISTENCY ISSUES TO RESOLVE BEFORE THE HEARING

1. **Marketing metrics discrepancy:** Sale Motion (85 contacted / 42 NDAs / 18 data room / 7 IOIs) vs. UCC statement (120 / 23 / 5). Reconcile via Langford Rae declaration; Tidewater can use this to attack process integrity.
2. **Docket citations:** Bid Procedures Order cited as Dkt. 142 in the Sale Motion and Dkt. 156 in the Tidewater Objection / Dkt. 148 in the UCC statement; Sale Motion cited as both Dkt. 187 and Dkt. 214 context. Confirm correct numbers in the reply.
3. **Cure-period exposure:** The 28-day cure within a 45-day window under MFA § 12.3 is adverse to the Debtor's litigation posture on Claim No. 247. Develop the accrued-interest cure-incompleteness theory and interest calculations now, for the claims objection, and keep it out of the Sale Hearing.
4. **Proof of Claim vulnerabilities (for later claims objection):** (a) Component 1 ("lost franchise fee *savings*") claims as damages the fees Tidewater *saved* by not paying — economically incoherent as stated and duplicative of lost profits; (b) lost profits ($851,111/yr "incremental brand profit") is an estimate without expert support and covers a period (post-Nov. 2024) already assuming rebranding completion; (c) Tidewater disclaims setoff despite the admitted ~$1.185M unpaid Q2/Q3 2023 obligations (cured pre-termination but relevant to interest); (d) the $851,111 × 4.5 calculation gives $3,829,999.50 — arithmetic fine but methodologically untested.

## V. RECOMMENDED RESPONSE STRATEGY (SUMMARY)

1. **File a consolidated omnibus reply** responding to each objection, leading with: (a) § 363(f) not implicated as to the MFA (Excluded Asset) and, alternatively, § 363(f)(4); (b) waiver/finality of bid-procedures arguments; (c) bid facially non-conforming and untimely.
2. **Propose a consensual carve-out in the Sale Order** (or argue it affirmatively if not accepted): preserve Claim No. 247 and all MFA-related claims against the estate; narrow ¶ 18 release; confirm the sale effects no transfer or extinguishment of any Tidewater franchise interest. This moots Objections 1, 4, and 6 without conceding anything on the merits of the termination dispute.
3. **Evidentiary support for the hearing:** declarations of Patricia Medina (recusal/negotiation lead), Langford Rae deal team (marketing breadth, recusal observation, reconciled outreach numbers, reasons non-bidders declined), and board secretary/minutes (May 30, 2024 unanimous vote with recusal).
4. **Leverage the UCC statement** as independent, court-recognized validation of the arm's-length process and the value of the deal.
5. **Emphasize timing:** DIP Maturity Milestone of September 30, 2024; any delay risks DIP default and Chapter 7 conversion, destroying unsecured recoveries (including, ironically, Tidewater's).
6. **Segregate the claims fight:** commit to adjudicating Claim No. 247 in the claims-objection process; avoid merits briefing on the termination at the Sale Hearing, where the 28-day-cure record is unhelpful.
7. **Anticipate the court's likely rulings:** Objections 2, 4, 5 overruled; Objection 3 overruled with heightened findings; Objection 6 resolved by narrowing ¶ 18; Objection 1 overruled or rendered moot by carve-out language.

**Overall risk assessment: LOW-to-MODERATE.** The sale should be approvable with tailored Sale Order language; the real exposure for the estate is not the sale but the subsequent allowance of Claim No. 247, where the Debtor's timeliness defense to the cure is weak under MFA § 12.3 and efforts should focus on damages-theory defects (duplicative Component 1; unsupported lost-profits estimate) and interest-incomplete-cure arguments.
