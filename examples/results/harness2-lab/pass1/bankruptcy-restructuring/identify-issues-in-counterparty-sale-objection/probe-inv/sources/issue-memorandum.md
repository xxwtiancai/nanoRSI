## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Tidewater raises six objections to the § 363 sale to Grandview ($158.5M: $141M cash + $17.5M assumed liabilities). Two objections (the § 363(f) / franchise-interest argument and the excluded-asset "cure rights" argument) are largely self-defeating because the APA expressly **excludes** the Terminated Franchise Agreements from the Purchased Assets, so the sale does not strip any Tidewater interest. Two objections (break-up fee; insider relationship) attack orders and disclosures already made and are procedurally and substantively weak. The late-bid argument fails because Tidewater's $160M bid is below the $164,255,000 court-approved minimum. The Paragraph 18 release objection is the only objection with genuine traction; the recommended strategy is to narrow the release language by consent order rather than litigate it. Overall recommendation: **oppose the objection in full; offer a targeted carve-out/remand of the release provision; support with UCC statement, Medina declaration, and Langford Rae declaration.**

## II. ARGUMENT-BY-ARGUMENT ANALYSIS AND RESPONSE

### Objection 1 — Sale cannot proceed free and clear under § 363(f) of Tidewater's franchise interest (Objection ¶¶ 30–35)

**Tidewater's position:** None of the five § 363(f) conditions is satisfied; Tidewater does not consent; its franchise rights (brand, reservation system, marketing program, technology) are "interests" under § 363(f) (citing *Folger Adam*, *TWA*).

**Analysis and response:** The argument is misdirected. Under APA § 2.2(d) (APA Summary § IV(d)), the Terminated Franchise Agreements are **Excluded Assets**; Grandview acquires no rights under the MFA, and the estate retains all disputes with Tidewater. The APA Summary states expressly that "the proposed sale free and clear under Section 363(f) … does not affect any franchise interest that Tidewater may claim to hold" because the MFA "is simply not part of the transaction." The free-and-clear clause of § 363(f) strips interests only from assets **being sold**; it cannot extinguish an interest in an asset that is not conveyed. Further, the Debtor's position (Sale Motion ¶¶ 20–22) is that the MFA was validly terminated pre-petition (November 15, 2023) and Tidewater holds at most a $6.8M general unsecured damages claim (POC No. 247) — a claim that rides through to the claims-objection process, unaffected by the sale. To the extent any residual ambiguity exists about whether trademarks being sold could be deemed sold subject to Tidewater rights, a belt-and-suspenders paragraph in the Sale Order confirming that nothing in the order affects POC No. 247 or any Tidewater claim against the estate fully moots the objection.

**Risk level: Low.** Response: argue lack of injury/mootness; add clarifying Sale Order language.

### Objection 2 — $4.755M (3.0%) break-up fee is excessive and chilled bidding (Objection ¶ 5, Ground Second)

**Tidewater's position:** The break-up fee and $1.5M expense cap (aggregate $6.255M, ~3.95%) chilled competition, evidenced by zero qualified competing bids.

**Analysis and response:** This attack is barred and substantively rebutted:
- **Finality/res judicata:** The Bid Procedures Order (¶¶ 12–14) approved the Break-Up Fee and Expense Reimbursement after notice and a May 17, 2024 hearing at which **no party objected** (Order ¶ F). The Court made express findings that the fee is reasonable, consistent with market practice, within the range approved in this District, and "will not unduly chill competitive bidding" (¶ 14). Tidewater filed no objection to bid procedures and cannot collaterally attack that order at the sale hearing.
- **Market evidence:** The marketing process (Sale Motion ¶¶ 24, 32) reached 85+ potential bidders, 42 NDAs/CIMs, 18 data-room participants, 7 preliminary IOIs; no bid exceeded $158.5M. Declining bidders cited capex needs, market conditions, and DIP timing — not the break-up fee.
- **Note a factual conflict** that opposing counsel may exploit: the Sale Motion reports 85 parties contacted / 42 NDAs / 18 data-room parties, while the UCC Statement (§ III.A) reports 120 contacted / 23 NDAs / 5 IOs. Neither figure suggests chilling, but the discrepancy should be reconciled (or clarified via the Langford Rae declaration) before the hearing.

**Risk level: Low.** Response: res judicata/estoppel; Court's express chilling findings; marketing statistics.

### Objection 3 — Insider relationship (Haines–Townsend) requires heightened scrutiny (Objection ¶¶ 26–29)

**Tidewater's position:** Gerald Haines (Grandview MD, APA signatory) is married to the sister of Debtor CEO Marcus Townsend; disclosure and recusal are inadequate; Medina reports to Townsend; Langford Rae was retained under Townsend before recusal.

**Analysis and response:** This is the objection requiring the most careful handling:
- **Full disclosure:** The relationship was disclosed in Sale Motion ¶ 47 and Bid Procedures Motion ¶ 23 (per Objection ¶ 28, which concedes disclosure). Grandview is not a statutory insider of the Debtor under § 101(31) — no employment, directorship, or equity relationship is alleged; a sibling-in-law connection between an employee of the buyer and the debtor's CEO does not make the **buyer** an insider.
- **Process safeguards:** Townsend recused from negotiations and from the May 30, 2024 board vote (Sale Motion ¶ 29); COO Medina led negotiations; independent banker Langford Rae ran the process; UCC counsel had data-room access, received all IOs, attended key negotiation sessions (Sale Motion ¶ 31; UCC Statement § III.A), and the UCC — after independent investigation — concluded the process was arm's length and the safeguards adequate.
- **Weaknesses to shore up before the hearing:** (i) Tidewater's point that Langford Rae was engaged pre-recusal under Townsend's leadership is not addressed in the materials read; be prepared with evidence that the banker's engagement was court-approved April 8, 2024 with UCC support (Sale Motion ¶ 23) and that Langford Rae had no prior relationship with Grandview or Haines (UCC Statement § III.A). (ii) Townsend's recusal is "self-reported" — a short declaration from Medina and a board-minute excerpt confirming recusal will defuse this. (iii) **Coverage gap:** the insider-disclosure email (insider-disclosure-email.eml) was unreadable; if it contains additional facts about the disclosure timeline or scope, they are not reflected here and should be reviewed by the trial team before the hearing.

**Risk level: Medium.** Response: disclosure + recusal + independent banker + UCC investigation; supply declaratory corroboration; consider an additional finding in the Sale Order that the sale was arm's length and in good faith under § 363(m).

### Objection 4 — APA exclusion of Terminated Franchise Agreements deprives Tidewater of cure rights under § 365 (Objection ¶ 5, Ground Fourth)

**Tidewater's position:** Excluding the MFA from Purchased Assets improperly strips Tidewater of § 365 cure/assumption rights.

**Analysis and response:** This inverts the prior argument and fails for the same reason. Section 365 applies only to contracts the debtor elects to **assume or reject**; Tidewater is not the debtor and has no unilateral right to force assumption of the MFA. Because the MFA was terminated pre-petition (November 15, 2023) per the Debtor (Sale Motion ¶ 20), the MFA is at most a rejected/terminated contract giving rise to a damages claim — exactly what Tidewater asserted in POC No. 247 ($6.8M general unsecured). If the termination was wrongful (Tidewater's view), Tidewater's remedy is an allowed claim for damages, not specific reinstatement of a franchise against a buyer who did not purchase it. The Debtor has represented it will object to POC No. 247 in due course (Sale Motion ¶ 21); that contest is preserved and unaffected by the sale.

**Risk level: Low.** Response: no standing to compel assumption; termination dispute is a claims-allowance matter; estate (not Buyer) retains all Tidewater-related liabilities as Excluded Liabilities (APA Summary § V).

### Objection 5 — Tidewater should be permitted to submit a late $160.0M competing bid (Objection ¶ 5, Ground Fifth)

**Tidewater's position:** A $160M bid exceeds the stalking horse price and maximizes value.

**Analysis and response:** Doubly deficient:
1. **Below the minimum bid:** The Bid Procedures Order ¶ 5(a) sets the minimum Qualified Bid at **$164,255,000** ($158.5M + $4.755M break-up fee + $1M initial overbid). Tidewater's $160M is short by $4.255M — and would yield the estate *less* net value than the Grandview deal even if accepted (the UCC Statement § V makes this point).
2. **Untimely:** The Bid Deadline was July 15, 2024, 5:00 p.m. (Order ¶ 6); late bids may be considered only "upon motion and for cause shown." Tidewater offers no cause — it was a known party in interest since May 1, 2024 (POC filing), served with the Bid Procedures Order, and offers no deposit ($15.85M required), financing proof, or APA markup (Order ¶ 5(b)–(f)). Tidewater's real objective appears to be leverage on its $6.8M claim, not a genuine purchase.
3. Delay risks the DIP Maturity Milestone (sale consummation by September 30, 2024); default could trigger acceleration and Chapter 7 conversion (Sale Motion ¶ 17; UCC Statement § II.B).

**Risk level: Low.** Response: enforce Bid Procedures Order as written; failure of every Qualified Bid requirement; prejudice to estate from delay.

### Objection 6 — Paragraph 18 of proposed Sale Order contains an impermissible non-consensual release (Objection ¶¶ 5, 23)

**Tidewater's position:** The release ("the Buyer, Seller, and their respective officers, directors, agents, and employees shall be released from any and all claims … arising out of or related to the Sale Transaction," quoted at Objection ¶ 23) would extinguish Tidewater's pre-petition claims including POC No. 247.

**Analysis and response:** This is Tidewater's **strongest** argument. Non-consensual third-party releases of claims against a debtor's estate (as opposed to lien/interest releases under § 363(f), or exculpation limited to good-faith conduct in the sale process) are disfavored and, following recent Supreme Court authority restricting nonconsensual third-party releases in plans, vulnerable in the sale context as well. Although the release as quoted is arguably limited to claims "arising out of or related to the Sale Transaction" (arguably not covering pre-petition wrongful-termination claims), the risk of an adverse ruling or a remand is real.

**Recommended strategy:** Do not litigate. Conform Paragraph 18 to standard practice: (a) limit the release/exculpation to claims arising from conduct in connection with the *sale process and the prosecution/participation in the Sale Motion*, (b) expressly carve out pre-petition claims and proofs of claim, including POC No. 247, which remain subject to allowance under § 502, and (c) retain the ordinary § 363(f) "interests in property" language as to Purchased Assets only. This moots the objection at no cost to the estate or Buyer (Tidewater's claims are unsecured claims against the estate, which retains them as Excluded Liabilities). The UCC has indicated it negotiated Sale Order modifications (Objection ¶ 10) — coordinate on this carve-out.

**Risk level: Medium-High (as drafted); Low after narrowing.**

## III. STRATEGY, WITNESSES, AND RELIEF REQUESTED

### A. Relief requested at the July 29, 2024 Sale Hearing

2. Approve the Sale and enter the Sale Order with (a) a clarifying paragraph that nothing in the order affects POC No. 247 or any claim of Tidewater against the estate, and (b) a narrowed Paragraph 18 release as described above.
