## I. Executive Summary

Tidewater Lodging Ventures, LLC ("Tidewater"), a disputed general unsecured creditor asserting a $6.8 million claim (Proof of Claim No. 247), objects to the proposed § 363 sale of substantially all assets to Grandview Capital Partners, LLC on six grounds. Our assessment:

- **Strongest defenses:** Ground 1 (§ 363(f) — franchise interest not among assets sold), Ground 4 (exclusion of Terminated Franchise Agreements defeats the § 365 cure-rights argument), Ground 5 (late $160M bid fails the $164,255,000 minimum qualified bid), and Ground 2 (break-up fee already approved by final, unobjected-to Bid Procedures Order).
- **Most risk:** Ground 3 (the Haines–Townsend insider relationship) and Ground 6 (the ¶ 18 release in the proposed Sale Order). Both are curable through order language and evidence; neither, standing alone, should defeat the sale.
- **A discrete merits issue on the underlying termination also merits early attention:** MFA § 12.3 provides a **45-day cure period**, and Tidewater's October 20, 2023 wire was sent **28 days** after the September 22, 2023 Default Notice — facially within the cure period. The Debtor's best contractual answer is that § 12.3 requires payment in full "together with any accrued interest as provided in Section 7.1" (1.5%/month), and the $1.185M wire did not include accrued interest. Separately, the November 15, 2023 Termination Letter made termination "effective immediately," which does not comply with § 12.4's requirement that the Termination Date be **not less than 10 nor more than 30 days** after the Termination Notice. These facts should be scrubbed before the hearing because they bear on the § 363(f)(4) "bona fide dispute" analysis and the eventual claim objection.

**Overall recommendation:** Oppose the objection on all six grounds; concede narrow clarifying language in the Sale Order (carve-out from the ¶ 18 release preserving Proof of Claim No. 247; express statement that nothing in the order adjudicates the MFA-termination dispute); do not reopen bidding.

## III. Argument-by-Argument Analysis and Response Strategy

### Ground 1 — Sale Does Not Satisfy § 363(f) as to Tidewater's Franchise Interest (Objection ¶¶ 30–33)

**Tidewater's position:** None of the five § 363(f) conditions is satisfied; Tidewater does not consent; its franchise rights are an "interest" under *Folger Adam*.

**Assessment: LOW RISK — largely moot by deal structure.**

**Response:**
1. **Threshold dispositive point:** Section 363(f) operates only to strip interests from property **being sold**. The Terminated Franchise Agreements, including the MFA, are expressly **Excluded Assets** under APA § 2.2(d) and are not on the Assumed Contracts Schedule. Nothing of Tidewater's is being sold free and clear of its interest; the APA Summary (§ IV) states expressly that § 363(f) "is not implicated with respect to Tidewater's alleged franchise relationship."
2. **In the alternative, § 363(f)(4) applies:** whatever interest Tidewater claims — under a live contract or as a wrongful-termination claim — is at minimum in **bona fide dispute** (the Debtor maintains termination was valid and intends to object to POC No. 247). Courts routinely permit free-and-clear sales where interests are in bona fide dispute.
3. **Damages claim, not property right:** even under Tidewater's own framing, its remedy is a **$6.8M general unsecured money claim** (POC No. 247) — a claim against the estate, not an interest in estate property. Under § 363(f)(5), a holder of a right to payment can be compelled to accept money satisfaction; alternatively, claims attach to proceeds with unchanged priority.
4. **Requested relief:** ask the Court to include clarifying language that the free-and-clear provisions do not expand the sale to Excluded Assets and do not adjudicate the validity of the MFA termination or POC No. 247.

**Evidence:** Medina and deal-counsel declarations confirming the exclusion; APA § 2.2(d) and Exhibit B.

### Ground 2 — Break-Up Fee Excessive and Chilled Bidding (Objection, *Second* ground)

**Assessment: LOW RISK — law of the case and finality.**

**Response:**
1. **The Bid Procedures Order (May 20, 2024) finally adjudicated this issue.** Paragraphs 12–14 contain express findings that the Break-Up Fee is reasonable, consistent with market practice, necessary to induce the stalking horse commitment, and "will not unduly chill competitive bidding." No party — including Tidewater, which received service — objected to the Bid Procedures Motion (Order, Finding F). The issue is waived.
2. **Aggregate protections (~3.95%) are within market norms** for transactions of this size; 3.0% break-up fees are routinely approved.
3. **Chilling argument fails factually:** the process was robust (85+ parties contacted per the Sale Motion; 120 per the UCC), with multiple NDAs, data-room participants, and IOIs. Non-bidders cited capex requirements, market conditions, and the DIP timeline — not the fee. *(Reconcile statistics — see § II.)*
4. **Fallback:** none needed; do not negotiate on this point.

### Ground 3 — Grandview Is an Insider; Heightened Scrutiny Required (Objection ¶¶ 26–29)

**Assessment: MODERATE RISK — the most factually resonant objection; manage with evidence and order findings.**

**Preliminary legal point:** Grandview is not a § 101(31) insider of the Debtor; the relationship is one step removed (spouse of the CEO's sister). A familial connection alone does not invalidate a sale — it invites heightened scrutiny of arm's-length process and price fairness.

**Response:**
1. **Full disclosure:** the relationship was disclosed in ¶ 47 of the Sale Motion and ¶ 23 of the Bid Procedures Motion — no concealment, a key factor courts weigh.
2. **Recusal corroborated, not merely self-reported:** board minutes of the May 30, 2024 meeting show Townsend recused from the APA deliberation and unanimous vote (Sale Motion ¶ 29). *Produce the minutes* and a declaration from a disinterested board member (not Townsend, not Medina).
3. **Independent checks on Medina:** the process ran alongside (a) an independent investment banker (Langford Rae — no prior relationship with Grandview or Haines, per the UCC Statement § III.A), (b) Debtor's counsel, and (c) the **UCC, whose counsel had full data-room access, received all IOIs, and attended key negotiation sessions** (Sale Motion ¶¶ 4, 31).
4. **The UCC's independent investigation and endorsement** (UCC Statement §§ III, V–VI) is powerful third-party validation; have UCC counsel appear and confirm.
5. **Banker-retention point:** Langford Rae was retained April 8, 2024 [Dkt. 79] with UCC support; be prepared to show what role, if any, Townsend had in the retention and that it was disclosed in the retention application.
6. **Price-fairness evidence:** multiple IOIs; no bid exceeded $158.5M; price is reasonable against $214.7M scheduled assets (much of it encumbered real property) and $267.3M scheduled liabilities. Secure a Langford Rae fairness/value declaration.
7. **Concession option:** offer Sale Order findings that, having reviewed the disclosure, recusal, and UCC oversight, the Court finds the transaction arm's-length and Grandview a good-faith purchaser under § 363(m). Costs nothing; defuses the objection.

### Ground 4 — APA Exclusion of Terminated Franchise Agreements Deprives Tidewater of § 365 Cure Rights (Objection, *Fourth* ground)

**Assessment: LOW RISK — legally inverted.**

**Response:**
1. **Tidewater has the argument backwards.** Section 365 cure provisions are triggered only when the debtor **assumes** a contract. The Debtor is not assuming the MFA; there is no assumption, no assignment, and no § 365(b) cure obligation running to or from Tidewater.
2. **Tidewater's remedy is a claim, not contract reinstatement.** The Debtor contends the MFA was validly terminated pre-petition (Nov. 15, 2023); there is no executory contract to assume or reject. Tidewater's rights are fixed by POC No. 247 and will be adjudicated in claims administration — exactly as the Sale Motion (¶ 22) represents: the exclusion "ensures that the Sale does not affect Tidewater's ability to assert claims against the estate through the claims allowance process."
3. **No forced reinstatement:** even if termination were wrongful, Tidewater cannot use the bankruptcy to compel specific performance of a franchise relationship where the brand, marks, and system are being sold to a third party; its remedy is damages for the remaining term — which is precisely what it claimed.
4. **Requested relief:** none beyond the clarifying findings described under Ground 1.

**Caution:** do not overstate the termination's merits at the sale hearing; rely only on the existence of a genuine dispute (§ 363(f)(4)). See § IV.

### Ground 5 — Tidewater's Late $160.0M Competing Bid Should Be Accepted (Objection, *Fifth* ground)

**Assessment: LOW RISK — categorically deficient.**

**Response:**
1. **Facially below the minimum qualified bid.** The Bid Procedures Order ¶ 5(a) sets the minimum Qualified Bid at **$164,255,000**. Tidewater's $160M is short by **$4,255,000** — nearly exactly the Break-Up Fee the estate would owe Grandview on an alternative sale — so it delivers no incremental value and less net value after delay and transaction costs.
2. **Untimely and non-compliant.** The Bid Deadline was July 15, 2024, 5:00 p.m. ET (Order ¶ 6); late bids "shall not be considered … for any purpose" absent a motion and cause shown. Tidewater made no motion, showed no cause, and has not offered the $15.85M deposit (¶ 5(b)), marked-up APA (¶ 5(c)), or committed-financing proof (¶ 5(d)). Tidewater is a hotel operator with a disputed $6.8M claim; demand proof of capacity to close $141M in cash.
3. **Finality and the DIP Milestone:** reopening bidding would jeopardize the September 30, 2024 DIP Maturity Milestone, risking DIP default and conversion to Chapter 7 — catastrophic to unsecured creditors (Sale Motion ¶ 17; UCC Statement § II.B).
4. **UCC opposition** to any late bid (UCC Statement § V) reinforces the estate-interest framing.
5. **Estoppel/sandbagging:** Tidewater received notice of the bid procedures, raised no objection, and now seeks to relitigate process terms after they proved disadvantageous to it.

### Ground 6 — ¶ 18 of the Proposed Sale Order Contains an Impermissible Non-Consensual Release (Objection ¶¶ 5 (*Sixth*), 23)

**Assessment: MODERATE RISK — the objection most likely to draw judicial sympathy; fix by narrowing.**

**Analysis:** As quoted in the Objection (¶ 23), ¶ 18 releases the Buyer, Seller, and their respective officers, directors, agents, and employees from "any and all claims … arising out of or related to the Sale Transaction." Read literally, "related to" could sweep in the Tidewater MFA dispute (which APA Exhibit D lists as related litigation). Non-consensual third-party releases are disfavored and, following *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma* (U.S. 2024), vulnerable.

**Response strategy:**
1. **Clarify, don't fight.** The release was intended as a standard sale-transaction release (claims arising from the conduct of the sale itself), not a release of pre-petition claims. Revise ¶ 18 to:
   - limit the release to claims **arising out of or relating to the conduct of the marketing, negotiation, and consummation of the Sale Transaction**;
   - expressly **carve out** pre-petition claims, including POC No. 247, and state that nothing in the Sale Order releases, extinguishes, or affects Proof of Claim No. 247 or Tidewater's rights in claims-objection proceedings.
2. **The concession costs the estate nothing** — the Debtor has consistently represented (Sale Motion ¶ 22) that the Tidewater dispute "will be resolved in the claims allowance process" — and it removes Tidewater's most meritorious-looking argument while fully preserving the claim objection.
3. Coordinate revised language with Grandview's counsel (Bracken Holt Steadman LLP) and the UCC before the hearing.

## IV. Cross-Cutting Issue: Merits of the MFA Termination (Affects Grounds 1 and 4)

Not to be litigated at the sale hearing, but requiring attention now:

- **Cure period arithmetic:** MFA § 12.3 provides **45 days** from the Default Notice (Sept. 22, 2023) — through approximately **November 6, 2023**. Tidewater's $1.185M wire on **October 20, 2023** (day 28) was facially timely. The Objection's characterization is arithmetically correct.
- **Debtor's best response:** § 12.3 requires cure "in full of all amounts then due and owing, **together with any accrued interest as provided in Section 7.1**" (1.5% per month on late amounts). The October 20 wire covered only the $1.185M principal; if accrued interest remained unpaid, the default was arguably uncured. Verify against receipt records and quantify. *Caution:* a small unpaid interest sum may strike a court as an inequitable basis to terminate a ten-year franchise agreement.
- **Procedural defect risk:** § 12.4 requires the Termination Notice to specify a Termination Date "**not less than ten (10) days and not more than thirty (30) days after the date of the Termination Notice**." The November 15, 2023 Termination Letter was "effective immediately" (Objection ¶ 16; POC § 3), which does not comply. Assess whether any prior or subsequent compliant notice exists.
- **Hearing posture:** rely on **§ 363(f)(4) bona fide dispute** (genuine dispute only — Tidewater's own alternative framings in POC No. 247 confirm dispute), not on an assertion that termination was clearly valid. For the future claim objection, gather: the Default Notice, wire records and interest computations, the Termination Letter with delivery proof, and any correspondence between October 20 and November 15, 2023 regarding accrued interest.

## VI. Bottom-Line Recommendation

1. **Oppose** all six objection grounds.
2. **Concede narrow Sale Order language:** (a) narrowed ¶ 18 release with an express carve-out for Proof of Claim No. 247; (b) findings that the Terminated Franchise Agreements are Excluded Assets and that the Sale Order does not adjudicate the validity of the MFA termination or Tidewater's claim.
3. **Do not reopen bidding:** Tidewater's $160M proposal is below the $164,255,000 minimum, untimely, unsupported by deposit or financing proof, and net-negative to the estate.
4. **Invest now** in (a) the arm's-length record (board minutes, independent declarations, UCC participation) and (b) the termination-merits file (interest calculation under MFA §§ 7.1/12.3; § 12.4 notice mechanics) for the upcoming objection to POC No. 247.
5. **Reconcile marketing statistics** between the Sale Motion and the UCC Statement via a Langford Rae declaration before the July 29 hearing.
