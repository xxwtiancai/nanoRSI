## III. ISSUE-BY-ISSUE ANALYSIS

### Ground 1 — Sale cannot proceed free and clear of Tidewater's franchise interest under § 363(f) (Obj. ¶¶ 30–33)

**Contention:** None of the five § 363(f) conditions is satisfied as to Tidewater's franchise interest; Tidewater does not consent.

**Counter-arguments / supporting evidence:**
- **Facially dispositive structural answer:** The MFA and the four Terminated Franchise Agreements are **Excluded Assets under APA § 2.2(d)** and are not on the Assumed Contracts Schedule. Section 363(f) applies by its terms only to interests "in such property" — property being sold. Because none of Tidewater's franchise interests are being conveyed, the free-and-clear provision does not operate on them. (APA Summary § IV: the mechanism of § 363(f) "is not implicated with respect to Tidewater's alleged franchise relationship because that relationship … is simply not part of the transaction.")
- The trademark/System IP being sold (APA § 2.1(d)) is the Debtor's own property, subject only to the Waverly lien (APA § 4.14); Tidewater holds no lien or ownership interest in the Marks — only contract-use rights under a terminated agreement, which are not conveyed.
- Tidewater's remedy is its $6.8M Proof of Claim, which is unaffected: all liabilities arising from the MFA/Tidewater dispute are Excluded Liabilities retained by the estate (APA Summary § V), and liens/interests attach to proceeds in the same priority (APA Summary § I).
- **Anticipate Tidewater's strongest rejoinder:** that selling the trademarks/System to Grandview functionally destroys its franchise rights (brand, reservation system) even if the MFA itself is excluded. Responses: (a) the MFA was terminated pre-petition (Nov. 15, 2023) and, on the Debtor's position, Tidewater has no live use rights; (b) if Tidewater believes the termination was wrongful, its remedy is damages — exactly how Tidewater itself framed it in POC No. 247 (a $6.8M **general unsecured claim** for wrongful termination, not a request to compel continued franchise performance). Tidewater cannot simultaneously claim wrongful termination (a money claim) and a continuing non-monetary "interest" that blocks a sale.

**Risk assessment:** LOW.

**Recommended strategy:** Oppose. Offer confirmatory Sale Order language: "Nothing in this Order or the Sale affects, releases, or extinguishes any claim of Tidewater Lodging Ventures, LLC, including Proof of Claim No. 247, or any rights Tidewater may assert in the claims allowance process with respect to the Master Franchise Agreement dated June 1, 2018, which agreement and the franchise agreements thereunder are Excluded Assets under the APA."

### Ground 2 — $4.755M break-up fee (3.0%) is excessive and chilled bidding (Obj. ¶ 5, Ground Second)

**Contention:** The 3.0% break-up fee is excessive; the absence of qualified competing bids evidences chilling.

**Counter-arguments / supporting evidence:**
- **Law of the case / failure to object below:** The Break-Up Fee, the $1.5M Expense Reimbursement, and the aggregate $6.255M (≈3.95%) protections were **expressly approved** by this Court in the Bid Procedures Order (¶¶ 12–14) after notice and a May 17, 2024 hearing at which **no party objected** (Finding F). The Court made specific findings of reasonableness, market consistency, non-chilling, and benefit to the estate (¶ 12 findings (i)–(iv); ¶ 14). Tidewater was served (Findings F; ¶ 2) and neither objected nor sought reconsideration; relitigating approved protections at the sale hearing is improper.
- **3.0% is within market range** (courts routinely approve fees up to 3–4% plus expense reimbursement; the aggregate here is 3.95%).
- **No chilling inference:** the Sale Motion documents outreach to 85+ parties, 42 NDAs/CIMs, 18 data-room participants, 7 preliminary IOIs, and multiple follow-ups; declining bidders cited **capex requirements, competitive market conditions, and DIP timing** — not the break-up fee (Sale Motion ¶¶ 24, 26, 32). Caution: the UCC Statement reports different funnel figures (120 contacted / 23 NDAs / 5 IOIs — UCC § III.A) versus the Sale Motion (85/42/18/7); reconcile these through a Langford Rae declaration to preempt a credibility attack on the process narrative.
- The fee is payable **only** upon a sale to another bidder and is subordinate to Waverly's DIP claims (Bid Procedures Order ¶ 12(a), (c)).

**Risk assessment:** LOW.

**Recommended strategy:** Oppose, relying on Bid Procedures Order ¶¶ 12–14 and Finding F. File a short Langford Rae supplemental declaration reconciling the outreach figures and confirming the reasons non-bidders declined.

### Ground 3 — Grandview is an insider; heightened scrutiny required (Obj. ¶¶ 26–29)

**Contention:** Gerald Haines (Grandview Managing Director, deal lead and APA signatory) is the brother-in-law of Debtor CEO Marcus Townsend (Haines is married to Catherine Haines née Townsend, Townsend's sister); disclosure and recusal are inadequate; heightened scrutiny applies.

**Counter-arguments / supporting evidence:**
- Grandview is **not a statutory insider** of the Debtor under § 101(31): it is not a director, officer, or governing-body appointee of the Debtor, nor (on this record) an affiliate of such a person — the connection is familial, between a buyer employee and a debtor officer, not between buyer and debtor. At most this is a non-statutory insider / relationship-of-influence argument.
- Even assuming heightened scrutiny, the Debtor can satisfy it: (a) **voluntary, timely disclosure** in Sale Motion ¶ 47 and Bid Procedures Motion ¶ 23 (Obj. ¶ 28 concedes disclosure); (b) **complete recusal** of Townsend from negotiations and from the unanimous May 30, 2024 board vote (Sale Motion ¶ 29); (c) COO Medina — with the company since 2016, COO since 2019 (Sale Motion ¶ 11) — led the process at the board's direction; (d) **Langford Rae**, an independent hospitality-focused banker with no prior relationship to Grandview or Haines (UCC § III.A), ran a broad marketing process and managed all bidder communications (Sale Motion ¶ 33); (e) the **UCC independently investigated** the process — including data-room logs and direct consultation with Langford Rae — and found it arm's length (UCC §§ I, III.A); (f) Grandview's price was the **highest of seven IOIs** (Sale Motion ¶¶ 26, 30, 32).
- **Address Tidewater's strongest sub-arguments directly:**
  - *"Recusal is self-reported and unverified."* Cure with evidence: declarations from Medina and a Langford Rae deal-team leader confirming Townsend had no role in or visibility into negotiations, bid evaluation, or APA terms; board minutes of the May 30, 2024 recused vote.
  - *"Medina reports to Townsend."* Cure with evidence: the board resolution formally designating Medina as sale-process lead answerable to the board (not the CEO); testimony that negotiation direction came from the board and was informed by Langford Rae's advice.
  - *"Langford Rae was engaged under Townsend's leadership before the recusal."* Weakest Tidewater point — Langford Rae is independent of Grandview/Haines (UCC § III.A), and its retention was approved by the Court on April 8, 2024 **with UCC support** (Sale Motion ¶ 23); engaging the banker does not taint the banker's process.
  - *"No competing bids suggest a rigged process."* Rebut with the market's independent reasons for declining (capex, market conditions, DIP timing — Sale Motion ¶ 32) and the seven-IOI competition that set the price.
- **Caution:** do not overstate. If the unreviewed insider-disclosure email shows late, incomplete, or inconsistent disclosure, this ground escalates quickly. Review the email and the docket disclosure chronology before the hearing.

**Risk assessment:** MODERATE (reputational/optics risk; curable with evidence and order language). This is the ground most likely to draw judicial questioning.

**Recommended strategy:** Oppose, supported by live testimony (Medina; Langford Rae) and documentary corroboration (board minutes/resolution). Propose Sale Order findings that the process was arm's length, disclosure was timely and complete, and the price is fair and reasonable based on the marketing record and the UCC's investigation.

### Ground 4 — APA's exclusion of the Terminated Franchise Agreements deprives Tidewater of § 365 cure rights (Obj. ¶ 5, Ground Fourth)

**Contention:** Excluding the MFA from the Purchased Assets improperly strips Tidewater of its § 365 cure rights.

**Counter-arguments / supporting evidence:**
- **§ 365 cure rights run in favor of the estate's counterparty, not against it.** If a contract is assumed and assigned, § 365(b)(1) requires the *estate/assignee* to cure defaults owed **to the counterparty**. Tidewater, as franchisee/counterparty, would be the *beneficiary* of cure, not a party "deprived" of cure rights. The objection's framing is doctrinally confused.
- Nothing in the sale forecloses § 365: the estate retains the Terminated Franchise Agreements as Excluded Assets, and the Debtor remains free to assume, reject, or litigate them. If Tidewater contends the MFA was never validly terminated, that question will be adjudicated in connection with Proof of Claim No. 247 or any assumption/rejection decision — precisely the outcome the exclusion preserves (Sale Motion ¶ 22; APA Summary § IV).
- Excluding a disputed, allegedly-terminated franchise agreement from a going-concern sale is a **sound business judgment**: it avoids embedding a $6.8M disputed claim in the transaction and avoids any suggestion the buyer is assuming Tidewater-related liabilities (none are assumed — APA Summary § V).

**Risk assessment:** LOW.

**Recommended strategy:** Oppose; in the reply, invite the Court's confirmation that nothing in the Sale Order affects any § 365 rights or the eventual allowance or disallowance of POC No. 247.

### Ground 5 — Tidewater should be permitted to submit a late $160.0M competing bid (Obj. ¶ 5, Ground Fifth)

**Contention:** A $160.0M late bid exceeds the stalking horse bid and would maximize value.

**Counter-arguments / supporting evidence — this ground is facially deficient on three independent grounds:**
1. **Below the minimum Qualified Bid.** The Minimum Qualified Bid is **$164,255,000** = $158,500,000 + $4,755,000 Break-Up Fee + $1,000,000 Initial Overbid Increment (Bid Procedures Order ¶ 5(a)). Tidewater's $160.0M bid is **$4,255,000 short** — it would leave the estate *worse off* than the Grandview sale after payment of the Break-Up Fee triggered by a sale to another bidder (¶ 12(a)). The UCC makes exactly this point (UCC § V). Tidewater's "exceeds the stalking horse bid" framing ignores the court-approved overbid arithmetic.
2. **Untimely.** The Bid Deadline was July 15, 2024, 5:00 p.m. ET, "time is of the essence"; late bids "shall not be considered … for any purpose … unless the Court, upon motion and for cause shown, orders otherwise" (¶ 6). Tidewater made no motion and shows no cause — no deposit, no marked-up APA, no proof of committed financing, and no irrevocable, contingency-free offer (¶¶ 5(b)–(f)).
3. **No ability-to-close showing.** Tidewater is a four-hotel franchisee with ~$31.6M annual gross room revenue (POC § 2; Obj. ¶ 2) and no showing of financing capacity for a $160M+ acquisition; Qualified Bids must be contingency-free with committed financing (¶ 5(d)–(e)). A bid from a creditor that would fund a purchase in part with a disputed $6.8M claim is plainly non-compliant.
- Any delay would jeopardize the **September 30, 2024 DIP Maturity Milestone** (Sale Motion ¶ 17), risking DIP default, loss of cash collateral, and Chapter 7 conversion — catastrophic to unsecured recoveries (UCC §§ II.B, IV, V).

**Risk assessment:** VERY LOW. This is Tidewater's weakest ground.

**Recommended strategy:** Oppose without qualification; do not negotiate over reopening the bidding for a sub-threshold bid, which would contravene the Bid Procedures Order's plain terms and undermine § 363(m) finality.

### Ground 6 — Paragraph 18 of the proposed Sale Order is an impermissible non-consensual release (Obj. ¶¶ 5, 23)

**Contention:** Proposed ¶ 18 ("Effective upon the Closing, the Buyer, Seller, and their respective officers, directors, agents, and employees shall be released from any and all claims, causes of action, or liabilities arising out of or related to the Sale Transaction") would extinguish Tidewater's $6.8M pre-petition claim.

**Counter-arguments / supporting evidence:**
- As drafted, the release is limited to claims "arising out of or related to the **Sale Transaction**," which arguably does not reach Tidewater's pre-petition wrongful-termination claim (a 2023 dispute predating the sale). But the phrase "any and all claims" and the inclusion of "officers, directors" invite exactly the attack Tidewater has made — non-consensual third-party releases are disfavored and, after *Purdue Pharma* (June 2024), nonconsensual third-party releases exceed § 105 authority. The timing is poor for defending broad release language.
- **This ground is curable and should be conceded.** Tidewater's underlying claim is a general unsecured claim that the Debtor intends to contest through a claims objection (Sale Motion ¶ 21); the sale does not need the release to succeed.

**Risk assessment:** MODERATE as drafted; LOW after amendment.

**Recommended strategy:** CONCEDE by amendment. Revise ¶ 18 to:

> "Effective upon the Closing, the Buyer and Seller are released from any and all claims, causes of action, or liabilities arising out of or relating to the Sale Transaction, the APA, or the sale process conducted in this case; **provided that nothing in this Order releases, extinguishes, or otherwise affects (a) any claim of any non-debtor party against the Debtor, the estate, or their property, including without limitation Proof of Claim No. 247 filed by Tidewater Lodging Ventures, LLC, which shall be resolved in the claims allowance process, or (b) any claim or cause of action of the estate, including avoidance actions, which are Excluded Assets retained by the estate.**"

This moots Ground 6 entirely at no cost to the transaction.

## IV. THE UNDERLYING TERMINATION DISPUTE — MERITS PREVIEW (for claims-objection planning, not the Sale Hearing)

The Sale Hearing should not adjudicate the termination dispute, but counsel should understand the merits posture because it frames settlement leverage:

- **Tidewater's strong points:** (a) MFA § 12.3 provides a **45-day cure period** from the September 22, 2023 Default Notice — running to approximately **November 6, 2023** — and the $1,185,000 wire on **October 20, 2023** (28 days) was facially within it, contrary to the Termination Letter's untimeliness assertion (Obj. ¶ 16) and to Sale Motion ¶ 20's statement that termination followed "expiration of the applicable cure period"; (b) even if termination were otherwise permissible, § 12.4 requires the Termination Notice to set an effective date **10–30 days after** the notice, whereas the November 15, 2023 termination was "effective immediately" (POC § 3) — a procedural defect.
- **Debtor's counter-points for the claims objection:** (a) § 12.3 requires cure of "all amounts then due and owing **together with accrued interest**" at 1.5%/month (§ 7.1); if the October 20 wire paid principal only, cure was incomplete; (b) the Default Notice covered Q2/Q3 2023 royalties and marketing only — other amounts (e.g., Q4 accruals; Technology Fees due the first business day of Q4 2023) may have remained uncured; (c) potential setoff/counterclaims for post-termination use of the Marks. Investigate the interest arithmetic and the fee ledger now.
- **Damages vulnerabilities in POC No. 247:** Component 1 ("Lost Franchise Fee Savings," $2,970,000) is facially incoherent — franchise fees are a **cost Tidewater would have paid**, not a loss it suffered; claiming avoided costs as damages inverts the damages measure and likely double-counts against the lost-profits component (lost profits should already be net of fees paid). Component 2 rests on an unsupported $851,111/year "brand premium" estimate from Tidewater's own statements, with expert analysis expressly reserved (POC § 4). A claims objection has substantial force on both components.
