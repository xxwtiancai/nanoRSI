## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY / SHORT ANSWERS

1. **12(b)(1) (subject matter jurisdiction):** Weak for DataCore; strong for us. Diversity is complete and the amount in controversy plainly exceeds $75,000 even excluding the consequential-damages figure DataCore attacks. The motion raises a merits-style "speculative damages" attack dressed in jurisdictional clothing. **Oppose; expect to win.**
2. **12(b)(2) (personal jurisdiction):** Strong for us on **specific** jurisdiction given the Atlanta office and Sousa's Georgia-based conduct. The alternative **general** jurisdiction theory (Compl. ¶ 17) is weak under *Daimler* and should be de-emphasized or abandoned. **Oppose, lead with specific jurisdiction.**
3. **12(b)(3) (venue / forum selection clause):** Our hardest ground. The MSA § 14.2 forum clause is mandatory and covers claims "arising out of or relating to" the Agreement, and Pinnacle does not allege fraud specific to the clause itself. However, 12(b)(3) is the **wrong procedural vehicle**: under *Atlantic Marine* (not cited by DataCore), a forum-selection clause does not render venue "improper" under § 1391; the remedy is § 1404(a) **transfer, not dismissal**. **Primary fallback: convert the remedy to transfer and/or argue Bremen unreasonableness; do not stake the case on defeating the clause outright.**
4. **12(b)(6):** Mixed. Counts II (fraud) and III (negligent misrepresentation) are well pleaded with the who/what/when/where specificity Rule 9(b) requires and the integration clause does not bar them. Count IV (GDTPA) has real vulnerabilities (Texas choice of law; injunctive-relief-only remedy). Count I (breach) faces a genuine limitations problem under MSA § 9.4 — this is DataCore's best 12(b)(6) argument — but accrual is a disputed fact question inappropriate for resolution on a 12(b)(6) motion, and there are tolling/repair-doctrine arguments from the record. Damages caps (§§ 11.1–11.2) are not dismissal grounds at all.

## ANALYSIS BY GROUND (in the Motion's own order)

### A. Rule 12(b)(1) — Subject Matter Jurisdiction

**DataCore's argument (fair summary):** The ~$4,466,000 damages figures — particularly the $1,200,000 consequential-damages number attributed to Greenleaf Accounting Group and the Harmon Ridge Capital lending impact — are speculative and inflated, casting doubt on the amount in controversy under 28 U.S.C. § 1332. (Mot. § IV.A.)

**Response.** The legal standard: on a facial 12(b)(1) attack, the court accepts the Complaint's allegations as true; the amount in controversy is tested by whether it appears to a legal certainty that the claim is really for less than the jurisdictional amount. **Strategic recommendation — oppose strongly:**

- Even **excluding** the $1,200,000 consequential figure and punitive damages entirely, the Complaint pleads over $2,335,000 in actual/reliance damages (Compl. ¶ 14) — nearly 30x the $75,000 threshold. Legal-certainty defeat is impossible on these pleadings.
- DataCore's "speculation" attack is a **merits argument mislabeled as jurisdictional**; it goes to proof of damages, not to jurisdiction. Flag this to the Court as an improper basis for dismissal.
- Candor point: the consequential-damages number is our thinnest damages category and would face a later challenge (summary judgment / Daubert-style scrutiny of Greenleaf's quantification). That does not affect 12(b)(1).

### B. Rule 12(b)(2) — Personal Jurisdiction

**DataCore's argument:** DataCore is at home in Austin; its officers, board, banking, engineering, support, and hosting are in Texas (or Virginia/Oregon data centers); *Daimler*/*Goodyear* foreclose general jurisdiction, and (per the Motion) Georgia contacts are insufficient for specific jurisdiction. (Mot. § IV.B; Whitford Decl. ¶¶ 4–8.)

**Standard:** Once the movant submits affidavits, plaintiff must make a prima facie showing of jurisdiction; federal courts in diversity apply the forum long-arm statute and Due Process; specific jurisdiction requires suit-related minimum contacts (*International Shoe*; *Burger King*; *Walker v. BuildDirect*, 733 F.3d 1001, 1005 (11th Cir. 2013)).

**Response — lead with specific jurisdiction; this is factually strong:**

- DataCore maintains a **permanent regional office in Atlanta** (100 Techwood Drive NW) staffed by ~14 employees servicing Southeast clients (Compl. ¶¶ 12, 16).
- **Victor Sousa, VP of Sales–Southeast, was based in and worked from the Atlanta office**, made the alleged misrepresentations from Georgia, and signed the MSA from Georgia (Compl. ¶¶ 12, 16, 25, 44). The alleged injury-causing conduct — the misrepresentations — is thus suit-related Georgia conduct directed at a Georgia plaintiff. This is a paradigmatic *Burger King* "purposeful availment plus suit-related conduct" case.
- The Whitford Declaration conspicuously does **not deny** the Atlanta office or Sousa's location; it recites Austin centrality instead. Jurisdictional-discovery fallback: if the Court is unpersuaded, request targeted jurisdictional discovery on Sousa's office location and the Atlanta office's functions.
- **Candor:** General jurisdiction (Compl. ¶ 17) will fail under *Daimler* — an Austin-headquartered, ~$210M company with one regional Georgia office is not "essentially at home" in Georgia. Recommend conceding or de-emphasizing that theory in the opposition to preserve credibility.
- **Note the Whitford Declaration defect:** it is counsel's declaration, largely on "information provided" by DataCore personnel (Whitford Decl. ¶¶ 3–4, 12) — competent evidence of Austin contacts but weak as a denial of Georgia contacts; we should argue it fails to rebut our prima facie showing.

### C. Rule 12(b)(3) — Improper Venue / Forum Selection Clause

**DataCore's argument:** MSA § 14.2 mandates exclusive resolution in Travis County, Texas courts; the clause is mandatory, within scope, and not the product of fraud or overreaching; under *Bremen*/*Scherk*/*Stewart* it is entitled to substantial deference. (Mot. § IV.C.)

**Response — two tracks:**

1. **Procedural track (strongest):** Under *Atlantic Marine Constr. Co. v. U.S. Dist. Court*, 571 U.S. 49 (2013) — which DataCore's motion **does not cite** — a forum-selection clause pointing to another federal forum does **not** make venue "improper" under 28 U.S.C. § 1391, and Rule 12(b)(3) dismissal is therefore the wrong vehicle; the clause is enforced, if at all, through a **§ 1404(a) motion to transfer**. Venue is otherwise proper in N.D. Ga. under § 1391(b)(2) (substantial part of events — the Georgia-made misrepresentations, negotiation, implementation, and injury; Compl. ¶ 18). A § 1404(a) transfer analysis requires DataCore to show the clause-validity/scope and public-interest factors — and under *Atlantic Marine* private-interest factors are deemed waived. So even on the correct vehicle, DataCore's burden is non-trivial.
2. **Substantive track (harder, argue in the alternative):**
   - **Scope:** The tort claims (Counts II–IV) allege **fraudulent inducement** — injury occurred pre-contract and independent of it; the SOW Capabilities Document was expressly *not* incorporated into the MSA (Compl. ¶ 39). Argue the fraud claims do not "arise out of or relate to" the Agreement. **Candor:** this is uphill — most courts read "relating to" broadly and these claims are intimately related to the MSA.
   - **Unreasonableness under *Bremen*:** Public-interest argument that Georgia has the stronger interest — the alleged deceptive conduct occurred in Georgia against a Georgia company; witness convenience (Nance, Lofton, Sousa, Baines).
   - **Fraud inducement of the contract generally:** Where a party is fraudulently induced to sign a contract containing a forum clause, courts (incl. *Scherk*-line cases) still enforce the clause **unless the fraud was directed at the clause itself**. Our Complaint pleads no clause-specific fraud. **Candid weakness — do not lead with a generic "fraud vitiates the clause" argument.**
   - Note also: Whitford Decl. ¶ 12 admits the clause was DataCore's **standard form, "not modified during the negotiation process"** — cuts both ways: undercuts "heavily negotiated" framing but also undercuts any overreaching argument.

**Strategic recommendation:** The realistic outcome range is (a) 12(b)(3) denial with leave to seek § 1404(a) transfer, or (b) transfer to W.D. Tex. (Austin Division — the Travis County federal court). Prepare the client for the possibility of a Texas forum; the merits arguments below largely survive transfer.

### D. Rule 12(b)(6) — Failure to State a Claim

Standard: *Twombly*/*Iqbal* plausibility; well-pleaded facts accepted as true; documents integral to the Complaint (the MSA, Exhibits A–B) may be considered; fraud must satisfy Rule 9(b) particularity (knowledge/scienter may be alleged generally).

#### 1. Count I — Breach of Contract

**DataCore's arguments (fair summary):** (i) time-barred by MSA § 9.4's one-year limitation running from when the claimant "knew or should have known" of the breach — Pinnacle knew by **August 2022**, filed **March 22, 2024**; (ii) damages capped by § 11.1 (~$432,000); (iii) consequential damages barred by § 11.2. (Mot. §§ I, IV.D.1.)

**Response:**

- **Limitations — DataCore's best 12(b)(6) argument; treat seriously.** Counters:
  - **Disputed accrual:** The precise date Pinnacle "knew or should have known" of the *breach* (as opposed to experiencing symptoms) is a fact question. The record shows escalating events — Aug. 2022 concerns, Nov. 2022 latency complaints, Dec. 5, 2022 formal notice, and DataCore's own CTO's **Jan. 12, 2023 written acknowledgment** that the platform did "not meet the capabilities described during the sales process" (Compl. ¶ 4) *(second-hand quotation — verify original before citing in a filing)*. Arguably Pinnacle could not know the breach was incurable until the **April 15, 2023 patch failed** to restore represented capability (~7,500 vs. 15,000 records; Mot. § II.C — DataCore's own brief concedes the shortfall). If accrual is April 2023 (or later), the March 22, 2024 filing is timely.
  - **Repair doctrine / equitable tolling:** DataCore's promises to fix (Jan. 12, 2023 email; April 2023 patch) support tolling or estoppel during the repair period.
  - **12(b)(6) impropriety:** Where contractual accrual depends on disputed extrinsic facts, limitations cannot be resolved on the pleadings; the proper vehicle is summary judgment.
- **§§ 11.1–11.2 caps are not dismissal grounds.** A damages limitation does not eliminate a claim; it limits recovery and is appropriately adjudicated later. Moreover, under Texas law (and the *Italian Colors* authority DataCore itself cites, 99 F. Supp. 3d 1199), limitation-of-liability and consequential-damages waivers are generally **unenforceable against fraudulent-inducement liability**; to the extent the breach claim intertwines with the fraud, the caps are contestable. Also note § 11.1 caps at "fees paid … during the twelve (12) months preceding the event" — $931,000 was actually paid (Mot. § II.C), giving an alternative cap measure argument.
- **Candor:** If accrual is fixed at Aug./Nov./Dec. 2022, Count I is very likely time-barred. Consider pleading in the alternative any breach occurring within one year of filing (e.g., failure to provide the platform in "material conformity with the Documentation," MSA § 2.4(a), continuing through termination).

#### 2. Count II — Fraudulent Inducement

**DataCore's arguments:** Not pleaded with Rule 9(b) particularity/plausibility; generalized product dissatisfaction recast as fraud; § 14.7 integration clause plus Texas parol evidence rule forecloses reliance on pre-contractual statements; economic loss rule. (Mot. § IV.D.2.)

**Response:**

- **Particularity is satisfied:** The Complaint pleads speaker (Sousa; Baines), dates (Jan. 10, 2022 email; Feb. 14, 2022 SOW Capabilities Document; Jan. 18 and Feb. 7, 2022 demos), place (Sousa's Atlanta office; Georgia-directed communications), the exact statements ("out of the box with zero custom configuration"; "up to 15,000 concurrent … records with sub-second latency"; "native integration with 40+ payroll and HRIS platforms"), and falsity with specifics (required ~$85,000 custom API work; actual capacity ~4,000, later ~7,500 records). (Compl. ¶¶ 28–41, and breach/discovery allegations.) This is the opposite of generalized dissatisfaction.
- **Scienter/plausibility:** The **January 12, 2023 Subramanian email** (Compl. ¶ 4) is powerful — DataCore's own CTO admitting the platform had "performance limitations at scale that do not meet the capabilities described during the sales process." Rule 9(b) permits knowledge to be alleged generally; the admission plus the specially configured demo staging environment (Compl. ¶ 34) make intent plausibly pleaded. *(Verify email original.)*
- **Integration clause:** Under Texas law, fraudulent-inducement claims are **not** barred by a standard integration/merger clause or the parol evidence rule (*Italian Colors*, which DataCore cites, supports this; a disclaimer-of-reliance clause would be needed, and § 14.7 is a garden-variety integration clause, not an express reliance disclaimer).
- **Economic loss rule:** Texas's independent-injury exception applies where, as here, the fraud concerns the very quality/fitness inducing the contract and caused injury separate from disappointed expectations (the $85,000 custom API spend and lost alternatives). This is a debatable point — candidly our independent-injury theory is not airtight; preserve alternative framing.

#### 3. Count III — Negligent Misrepresentation

**DataCore's arguments:** Barred by integration clause and economic loss rule; no independent duty. (Mot. § IV.D.3.)

**Response:** Same particularity facts as Count II. On the economic loss rule, candidly this count is our **weakest tort claim** — Texas (and Georgia) courts frequently dismiss negligent misrepresentation claims between sophisticated commercial parties for purely economic loss absent an independent duty. Options: (a) argue independent injury (same as above); (b) consider whether to concede/deprioritize this count to strengthen the fraud count's credibility; or (c) if Georgia law applies (see § E below), Georgia's economic loss rule analysis may be more favorable to negligently-supplied-information theories.

#### 4. Count IV — Georgia UDTPA (O.C.G.A. § 10-1-370 et seq.)

**DataCore's arguments:** Duplicative; economic loss rule; Texas law governs. (Mot. § IV.D.4.)

**Response:**

- Not duplicative: statutory claim rests on the same deceptive conduct but provides distinct (injunctive) relief.
- **Candor — two real vulnerabilities:** (i) **Choice of law:** MSA § 14.3 selects Texas law for disputes "**arising under this Agreement**" — narrower than the § 14.2 "arising out of or relating to" forum language. If the UDTPA claim (and the tort claims) do not "arise under" the Agreement, Georgia choice-of-law rules may keep Georgia law — and the GDTPA — in play. But if the Court reads § 14.3 broadly, Texas law governs and the Georgia statute falls away. (ii) **Remedy:** the GDTPA provides injunctive relief, not damages; standing for injunctive relief is questionable where Pinnacle has terminated and no longer uses TalentBridge. Assess whether this count is worth defending; it may be a candidate for voluntary dismissal to streamline.

### E. Cross-Cutting — Choice of Law

DataCore argues § 14.3's Texas choice governs all counts. Response: § 14.3 covers only disputes "arising under" the MSA; fraudulent inducement and statutory claims arising from **pre-contractual** conduct fall outside "arising under," and under Georgia's lex loci delicti rules (misrepresentations made and relied on in Georgia), Georgia substantive law should govern the tort/statutory counts regardless. Candor: outcome varies with forum — a Texas court (if transferred) may be less receptive. Also note the motion's citation of **Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 2.725 (UCC four-year limitations)** implicitly concedes that if the UCC applies, the four-year period — not § 9.4's one year — may control the contract claim; press this inconsistency.

## STRENGTH ASSESSMENT (our view, candidly)

- **Strongest grounds for us:** 12(b)(1); 12(b)(2) specific jurisdiction; Rule 9(b) adequacy of the fraud count; §§ 11.1–11.2 are not dismissal grounds.
- **Weakest for us:** 12(b)(3)/forum clause on the merits (our best relief is the *Atlantic Marine* procedural point, which converts dismissal into, at worst, transfer); Count I limitations if accrual is fixed at Aug. 2022; Count III economic loss rule; Count IV choice of law and standing.
- **DataCore's single best argument:** MSA § 9.4 one-year limitations against Count I; mitigate with accrual/repair-doctrine arguments and the April 2023 patch timeline.
